30 × 60 concrete slab

Cubic yards, 40, 60 and 80 lb bags and rebar for a 30 by 60 foot slab, at 4, 5, 6 and 8 inches thick. Change any number in the calculator.

Quick answer: a 30 × 60 ft slab is 1,800 sq ft. At 4 in thick it takes 22.22 cubic yards of concrete, 24.44 with 10 % extra: order 24.5 yards of ready-mix or 1,100 bags of 80 lb. At 6 in thick it takes 36.67 yards with the extra.

Worked example: a 30 × 60 ft slab, 4 in thick

  1. Area: 30 × 60 = 1,800 sq ft. Perimeter for the forms: 2 × (30 + 60) = 180 ft.
  2. Volume: 1,800 sq ft × 4 in ÷ 12 = 600.0 cu ft, ÷ 27 = 22.22 cu yd.
  3. With 10 % extra for uneven ground and spillage: 24.44 cu yd. Ready-mix is ordered by the quarter yard: 24.5 cu yd.
  4. In bags: 660.0 cu ft ÷ 0.60 = 1,100 bags of 80 lb, or ÷ 0.45 = 1,467 bags of 60 lb. Over a cubic yard, so ready-mix is usually less work than 1,100 bags.
  5. Rebar at 16 in on centre: 24 bars of 59.5 ft and 46 bars of 29.5 ft, cut from 170 sticks of 20 ft #4, 1,992 lb of steel.

Result: order 24.5 cu yd of ready-mix or 1,100 bags of 80 lb, and 170 sticks of #4 rebar.

Questions

How many yards of concrete do I need for a 30x60 slab?

22.22 cubic yards at 4 in thick, 27.78 at 5 in and 33.33 at 6 in, before waste. With 10 % extra, order 24.5 yards for a 4 in slab and 36.75 for a 6 in slab.

How many bags of concrete for a 30x60 slab?

At 4 in thick with 10 % extra: 1,100 bags of 80 lb, 1,467 bags of 60 lb or 2,200 bags of 40 lb. Over a cubic yard, so ready-mix is usually less work than 1,100 bags.

How much rebar do I need for a 30x60 slab?

A #4 grid at 16 in on centre takes 170 sticks of 20 ft (2,982 ft of bar, 1,992 lb). At 12 in on centre it is 223 sticks, at 24 in 114. Use the spacing on your plans.

How thick should a 30x60 slab be?

That depends on what it carries and on the ground under it, so take it from your plans, your engineer or the local building department. The table shows the concrete for 4, 5, 6 and 8 in so you can price each.

How much extra concrete should I order?

Estimating guides commonly add 5–10 % for spillage and an uneven subgrade, more for complicated forms or pumping. Running short in the middle of a pour costs far more than a little left over.
