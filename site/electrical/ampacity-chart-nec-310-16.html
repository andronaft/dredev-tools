Ampacity Chart: NEC Table 310.16

Allowable ampacity of copper and aluminum conductors, 14 AWG to 500 kcmil, in the 60 °C, 75 °C and 90 °C columns, with correction factors and worked examples.

Quick answer: at 75 °C, copper carries 20 A on 14 AWG, 25 A on 12 AWG, 35 A on 10 AWG, 50 A on 8 AWG, 65 A on 6 AWG and 85 A on 4 AWG (NEC Table 310.16, 2023). Breakers on 14, 12 and 10 AWG copper are limited to 15, 20 and 30 A by NEC 240.4(D).

Checked 12 November 2026 by Andrii Zhukovskyi. Values from NFPA 70, National Electrical Code, 2023 edition, Table 310.16, as used in the Electrician Calc app.

Worked example: 60 A feeder, copper THHN, 75 °C terminations

  1. The load is 60 A, not continuous, so the breaker is 60 A.
  2. Terminations are rated 75 °C, so read the 75 °C copper column: 8 AWG is 50 A, too small; 6 AWG is 65 A.
  3. In aluminum the same column gives 4 AWG at 65 A.

Result: 6 AWG copper or 4 AWG aluminum on a 60 A breaker.

Worked example: 12 AWG THHN copper, six current-carrying conductors in one conduit at 40 °C

  1. THHN is a 90 °C conductor, so the correction starts from the 90 °C column: 12 AWG copper is 30 A.
  2. Ambient 36–40 °C, 90 °C conductor: multiply by 0.91.
  3. Four to six current-carrying conductors: multiply by 0.80.
  4. 30 × 0.91 × 0.80 = 21.8 A.
  5. The result may not exceed the 75 °C termination value (25 A), and 240.4(D) still limits 12 AWG copper to a 20 A breaker.

Result: 21.8 A after correction, enough for a 20 A circuit.

Questions

What is the ampacity of 12 AWG copper wire?

20 A at 60 °C, 25 A at 75 °C and 30 A at 90 °C in NEC Table 310.16. NEC 240.4(D) limits the breaker on 12 AWG copper to 20 A in most circuits.

What is the ampacity of 6 AWG wire?

Copper: 55 A at 60 °C, 65 A at 75 °C and 75 A at 90 °C. Aluminum: 40, 50 and 55 A.

Which temperature column do I use?

The lowest rating in the circuit: the conductor insulation and the terminals at both ends. NEC 110.14(C) sets the termination rules; many breakers and lugs are marked 75 °C. NM-B cable is held to the 60 °C column by NEC 334.80. The 90 °C column is mainly a starting point for correction factors.

What size wire for 100 amps?

3 AWG copper or 1 AWG aluminum at 75 °C. Dwelling services and main feeders may use the smaller sizes of NEC 310.12.

When do I have to derate?

When the ambient temperature is not 30 °C (86 °F), or when more than three current-carrying conductors share a raceway or cable. Multiply the table ampacity by the correction and adjustment factors below.

Where do these numbers come from?

NFPA 70, National Electrical Code, 2023 edition: Table 310.16, with the ambient correction factors of 310.15(B) and the adjustment factors of Table 310.15(C)(1). They are the values the Electrician Calc app uses. The code text is not reproduced here: read the full table, its notes and your local amendments in the code itself.
