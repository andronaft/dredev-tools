R-427A PT Chart

R-427A pressure–temperature chart in psig and °F/°C. R-22 retrofit for AC and refrigeration.

Quick answer: at 95 °F, R-427A is 200.3 psig as saturated liquid (bubble point) and 175.3 psig as saturated vapor (dew point); at 40 °F it is 76.6 psig and 61.2 psig. Use the vapor (dew) column for superheat and the liquid (bubble) column for subcooling.

Checked 12 November 2026 by Andrii Zhukovskyi. Every pressure on this page is a cell of the Arkema Forane® Pressure-Temperature Wall Chart, Americas (2019).

Questions

What is the pressure of R-427A at 95 °F?

200.3 psig as saturated liquid (bubble point) and 175.3 psig as saturated vapor (dew point), from the Arkema Forane® Pressure-Temperature Wall Chart, Americas (2019).

What is the pressure of R-427A at 40 °F?

76.6 psig liquid (bubble) and 61.2 psig vapor (dew).

How do I read the bubble and dew columns for R-427A?

A blend boils over a range of temperatures at one pressure. The bubble point is where the liquid starts to boil and the dew point is where the last liquid has boiled off. Read the suction pressure in the vapor (dew) column to get the saturation temperature for superheat, and the liquid-line pressure in the liquid (bubble) column for subcooling. For R-427A the two differ by about 10.4 °F at a 40 °F dew point. More on bubble and dew points.

Why are some readings in inches of mercury?

Below atmospheric pressure a gauge reads vacuum. The chart prints those cells as inches of mercury (″ Hg) below one atmosphere instead of psig.

Can I print this chart?

Yes. Use the Print button: the page prints as a clean table without the menus, with the source and the check date at the bottom.

Where does the data come from?

Arkema Forane® Pressure-Temperature Wall Chart, Americas (2019), copied cell for cell every 5 °F. The calculator interpolates between the printed points along the vapor-pressure curve. At 150 °F the 2025 edition of Arkema's chart prints 456.8 and 467.4 psig for R-427A. Those are the R-404A and R-507A cells of the same row, and a vapor pressure above the liquid pressure cannot be right. This page uses the 2019 edition, which prints 418.9 and 388.8 psig; every other R-427A row is the same in both editions.
