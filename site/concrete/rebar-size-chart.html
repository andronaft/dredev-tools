Rebar size chart and weight calculator

Diameter, area and weight of every common US, European and Canadian reinforcing bar, and the weight of the bars on your list.

Quick answer: #3 rebar is 3/8 in (9.5 mm) and weighs 0.376 lb per foot, #4 is 1/2 in (12.7 mm) and 0.668 lb/ft, #5 is 5/8 in (15.9 mm) and 1.044 lb/ft: the bar number is the diameter in eighths of an inch up to #8. Metric bars are named in mm (Ø12 = 12 mm, 0.888 kg/m); Canadian 15M is 16.0 mm, 1.578 kg/m.

Worked example: the weight of 20 bars of #4, 20 ft long

  1. Total length: 20 bars × 20 ft = 400 ft.
  2. Weight per foot of #4: area π/4 × 0.5² = 0.196 in², × 0.2836 lb/in³ steel × 12 in = 0.668 lb/ft.
  3. Total: 400 ft × 0.668 = 267 lb, or 121 kg.
  4. In metric: 50 bars of Ø12, 6 m long = 300 m × 0.888 kg/m = 266 kg.

Result: 20 × 20 ft of #4 weighs about 267 lb; 50 × 6 m of Ø12 weighs about 266 kg.

Questions

How much does #4 rebar weigh?

0.668 lb per foot, the figure ASTM A615 prints as 0.668. A 20 ft stick weighs about 13.4 lb and a 10 ft stick about 6.7 lb.

What size is #5 rebar in mm?

15.9 mm (5/8 in). In ASTM's soft-metric marking the same bar is #16; the nearest European bar by area is Ø16 and the nearest Canadian bar 15M.

What do 10M, 15M and 20M mean?

They are Canadian bar sizes under CSA G30.18, named after the rounded diameter: 10M is 11.3 mm, 15M 16.0 mm and 20M 19.5 mm across, with nominal areas of 100, 200 and 300 mm².

How do I calculate the weight of rebar?

Area × density × length. For a bar of d mm, mass in kg/m = d² ÷ 162 (π/4 × d² mm² × 7,850 kg/m³). For a US bar of d inches, lb/ft = 2.67 × d². Multiply by the total length of bar.

Is #4 rebar the same as 12 mm?

No. #4 is 12.7 mm and 127 mm²; Ø12 is 113 mm², about 11 % less steel. Ø14 has 154 mm². Changing bar size needs the engineer's approval.

Why does my supplier's chart show slightly different weights?

Standards print rounded nominal areas and weights, and some charts compute from them. The table here computes from the nominal diameter, so its values can differ in the last digit, by up to 0.6 % for Canadian bars. Real bars also vary within the mill tolerance.
