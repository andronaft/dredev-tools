Box fill: NEC 314.16 chart and examples

The volume each conductor needs, what counts as a conductor, how many wires common metal boxes take, and two worked examples.

Quick answer: each conductor needs 2.00 in³ of box for 14 AWG, 2.25 in³ for 12 AWG and 2.50 in³ for 10 AWG (NEC Table 314.16(B)(1), 2023 edition). Count one for each insulated conductor, one for all internal clamps, one for a fixture stud or hickey, two for each switch or receptacle and one for up to four ground wires, then multiply by the volume for the wire size. Two 12/2 cables and a receptacle in a box with clamps come to 8 × 2.25 = 18 in³.

Add the allowances and multiply by the volume for the wire size. The box volume must be at least that: Table 314.16(A) for standard metal boxes, the marked volume for plastic boxes, plus the marked volume of any extension or plaster ring.

Worked example: a receptacle fed by two 12/2 cables, metal box with internal clamps

  1. Insulated conductors: two cables with a hot and a neutral each, 4 allowances.
  2. Internal clamps: 1. Receptacle: 2. Two ground wires: 1.
  3. Total: 4 + 1 + 2 + 1 = 8 allowances.
  4. All wires are 12 AWG at 2.25 in³ each: 8 × 2.25 = 18 in³.

Result: 18 in³. The smallest standard metal box is a 4 in square × 1¼ in box (18 in³).

Worked example: a junction box with five 12/2 cables spliced, internal clamps, no device

  1. Insulated conductors: five cables with a hot and a neutral each, 10 allowances.
  2. Internal clamps: 1.
  3. Five ground wires: 1 for the first four and ¼ for the fifth, 1¼ allowances.
  4. Total: 10 + 1 + 1¼ = 12.25 allowances. 12.25 × 2.25 = 27.56 in³.

Result: 27.56 in³. The smallest standard metal box is a 4 11/16 in square × 1½ in box (29.5 in³).

Questions

How many 12 AWG wires can be in a 4 inch square box?

A 4 in square × 1½ in box is 21 in³ and takes 9 conductors of 12 AWG; the 2⅛ in deep one is 30.3 in³ and takes 13. That is with nothing else in the box: a device counts as two conductors, clamps as one and the grounds as one.

How many 14 AWG wires fit in a single-gang box?

A 3 × 2 × 3½ in metal device box is 18 in³: 9 conductors of 14 AWG, or 8 of 12 AWG, before devices, clamps and grounds are counted. For a plastic box use the volume stamped inside it.

Do ground wires count in box fill?

Yes, but together: up to four ground wires count as one conductor, and each one over four adds a quarter (NEC 314.16(B)(5)).

Why do older guides count all the grounds as one?

Before the 2020 edition every ground in the box shared a single allowance. Since NEC 2020 each ground over four adds a quarter, so the junction box in the second example needs 12.25 allowances (27.56 in³) where an older guide says 12 (27 in³).

Do pigtails and wire nuts count?

No. A pigtail that starts and ends inside the box is not counted, and neither are wire connectors.

Does a switch or receptacle count?

Yes: each device on one yoke counts as two conductors of the largest size connected to it.

What if the wires are different sizes?

The code takes each item at the size of the conductor it belongs to: a device at the largest wire connected to it, clamps at the largest wire in the box. This page and the app use one size for everything, the largest wire in the box, which never gives a smaller box than the code.

Where do these numbers come from?

NFPA 70, National Electrical Code, 2023 edition: 314.16(B) and Table 314.16(B)(1) for the volume allowances, Table 314.16(A) for the metal box volumes, as held in the Electrician Calc app. The code text is not reproduced here.
