What is particular about R-134a

Low pressures, and a vacuum below -15 °F

R-134a works at the lowest pressures of the refrigerants with their own page here. Its chart reaches 0.0 psig at -15 °F; colder than that the cells are printed in inches of mercury below atmospheric pressure: 3.7″ Hg vacuum at -20 °F and 14.8″ Hg vacuum at -40 °F.

The calculator takes gauge pressure in psig. To enter a vacuum reading, multiply the inches of mercury by 0.491 and type the result with a minus sign: 10″ Hg is 4.9 psi below atmospheric, typed as -4.9, which is -30.3 °F for R-134a.

One substance, one column

R-134a is not a blend, so it has a single saturation temperature at each pressure and no glide. The same chart reading is used for superheat and for subcooling.

R-513A is close, with a chart of its own

Arkema lists R-513A as a replacement for R-134a, except in automotive AC (Arkema, 2025 Forane® Technical Digest). It runs a little higher: 40.1 psig against 35.0 psig at 40 °F, and 143.1 psig against 135.0 psig as 105 °F liquid. At 18 psig R-134a is at 19.4 °F and the dew point of R-513A is 13.8 °F, 5.6 °F apart. See R-513A vs R-134a.

Oil, class and what it replaced

Arkema names POE as the lubricant for R-134a, with PAG in automotive AC only, and lists R-134a as the replacement for R-12 (Arkema, 2025 Forane® Technical Digest). R-134a is in safety class A1 (lower toxicity, no flame propagation) (ASHRAE Standard 34 class as listed by US EPA SNAP, Substitutes in Residential and Light Commercial Air Conditioning and Heat Pumps (page updated 19 March 2026)); EPA gives it a 100-year GWP of 1,430 (US EPA, Technology Transitions GWP Reference Table (page updated 2 April 2026)).

R-134a worked examples and pressures

The readings below are a medium-temperature refrigeration example. They show the arithmetic; they are not charging targets.

Worked example: R-134a superheat

  1. Suction (low-side) gauge pressure: 18 psig.
  2. Saturation temperature at 18 psig in the chart: 19.4 °F.
  3. Suction line temperature near the service valve: 31 °F.
  4. Superheat = 31 − 19.4 = 11.6 °F.

Result: 11.6 °F of superheat.

Worked example: R-134a subcooling

  1. Liquid (high-side) gauge pressure: 135 psig.
  2. Saturation temperature at 135 psig in the chart: 105.0 °F.
  3. Liquid line temperature near the service valve: 95 °F.
  4. Subcooling = 105.0 − 95 = 10.0 °F.

Result: 10.0 °F of subcooling.

What should R-134a superheat and subcooling be?

There is no single correct number for a refrigerant. The target belongs to the equipment: a fixed-orifice (piston) system is charged to a target superheat from the manufacturer's charging table (target superheat calculator), and a TXV or EEV system to the target subcooling on the unit's data plate. Always use the manufacturer's numbers for the unit in front of you.

Questions

How do I enter a vacuum reading for R-134a?

As negative psig: inches of mercury × 0.491, with a minus sign. 10″ Hg of vacuum is typed as -4.9 and gives -30.3 °F.

At what temperature is R-134a at 0 psig?

At -15 °F on Arkema's chart (0.0 psig). Below that the suction side is in a vacuum.

Can I use the R-134a chart for R-513A?

No. At 18 psig R-134a is at 19.4 °F and the dew point of R-513A is 13.8 °F.

Which oil goes with R-134a?

Arkema names POE, with PAG in automotive AC only.

Where does the data come from?

Arkema Forane® Pressure Temperature Chart (PPC/SDC 05-2025). Between the printed 5 °F points the calculator interpolates along the vapor-pressure curve.
