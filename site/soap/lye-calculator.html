Lye calculator (NaOH)

Sodium hydroxide and water for your oils, with superfat and lye concentration. Always double-check before you weigh lye.

Quick answer: lye = Σ (oil grams × SAP) × (1 − superfat), with SAP converted from KOH to NaOH by 39.997 ÷ 56.106. For 700 g olive and 300 g coconut oil at 5 % superfat: 142 g NaOH and 289 g water at 33 % concentration.

Worked example: 1,000 g of oils, 70 % olive and 30 % coconut

  1. KOH equivalent: 700 g × 0.190 + 300 g × 0.257 = 210.1 g.
  2. Convert to NaOH: × 0.7129 = 149.8 g. With 5 % superfat: × 0.95 = 142.3 g.
  3. Water at 33 % lye concentration: 142.3 × 67 ÷ 33 = 289 g.

Result: 142 g NaOH and 289 g water for 1,000 g of oils.

Questions

How is the lye amount calculated?

Lye = Σ (oil grams × SAP value) × (1 − superfat) ÷ purity. SAP values are in grams of KOH per gram of oil and are converted to NaOH by molar mass (39.997 / 56.106).

What superfat should I use?

5 % is a common starting point for bar soap: a little oil stays unsaponified for a milder bar. Salt bars and 100 % coconut soaps often use 15–20 %.

Where do the SAP values come from?

From the SoapCalc oil list, cross-checked against published saponification values. Oils from different suppliers vary, so treat results as a starting point.
