Ammonia toxicity chart

How much of the ammonia your test kit reads is the toxic un-ionized form (NH₃), from your pH and water temperature, with the chart behind it.

Quick answer: free (un-ionized) ammonia = the kit reading × the NH₃ share for your pH and temperature. At 25 °C the share is 0.566 % at pH 7.0, 5.38 % at pH 8.0 and 12.5 % at pH 8.4, so the same 1 ppm reading is about ten times more toxic in a hard-water tank at pH 8 than in a soft one at pH 7.

The kit's number is total ammonia (NH₃ + NH₄⁺); each cell is the part of it that is free NH₃, in the same unit.

Worked example: 1 ppm on the kit, pH 8.2, 26 °C

  1. pKa at 26 °C: 0.09018 + 2729.92 ÷ (273.2 + 26) = 9.214.
  2. Share as NH₃: 1 ÷ (1 + 10^(9.214 − 8.2)) = 0.0882, or 8.82 %.
  3. Free ammonia: 1 ppm × 0.0882 = 0.088 ppm NH₃; the other 0.912 ppm is ammonium.
  4. The same reading at pH 7.0 is only 0.0061 ppm NH₃, 15 times less.

Result: about 0.088 ppm of free ammonia. Any ammonia reading in an established tank is a reason to act.

Questions

What is the difference between ammonia and ammonium?

In water, ammonia exists as un-ionized ammonia (NH₃), which passes through fish gills and is the toxic part, and as the ammonium ion (NH₄⁺), which is far less toxic. The balance between them depends on pH and temperature. Most aquarium test kits measure both together, as total ammonia.

How do I work out free ammonia from my test kit?

Multiply the kit's reading by the NH₃ percentage for your pH and temperature, divided by 100. 1 ppm at pH 8.0 and 25 °C is 1 × 5.38 ÷ 100 = 0.054 ppm NH₃. The calculator above does it for any reading.

Why does pH matter so much?

Each 1.0 pH unit raises the NH₃ share about ten times, and even 0.2 pH changes it by more than half again: at 25 °C it is 3.47 % at pH 7.8 and 5.38 % at pH 8.0. So measure pH with care and at the same time as ammonia.

Is ammonia safer in my soft-water tank?

Less of it is in the toxic form at low pH, so the same reading does less harm: at pH 6.6 and 26 °C only 0.242 % is NH₃. But a water change or a pH rise turns ammonium back into NH₃ at once, so a reading in an established tank still needs fixing.

What level of free ammonia is safe?

This page gives no safe level, on purpose. Tolerance varies with species, size and exposure time, and published limits such as US EPA's water quality criteria are written for wild rivers and lakes, not for aquarium fish. In a cycled tank the target is zero total ammonia.

Does it work for saltwater or reef tanks?

No. The equation is for fresh water. In sea water the NH₃ share at the same pH and temperature is lower, so this chart overstates it for a marine tank; use a seawater table for those.

My kit reads ammonia-nitrogen (NH₃-N). What do I do?

The same percentages apply. To express the result as NH₃ rather than as nitrogen, multiply by 17.03 ÷ 14.007 = 1.216.
