EV charger wire size by amps and distance

Breaker and copper wire for 32, 40, 48 and 80 amp Level 2 chargers on 240 V, with the 125 % continuous-load rule and the voltage drop from 25 to 200 ft.

Quick answer: 48 A charger: 60 A breaker and 6 AWG copper for runs up to 150 ft. 40 A charger: 50 A breaker and 8 AWG copper for runs up to 100 ft. 32 A charger: 40 A breaker and 8 AWG copper for runs up to 100 ft. 80 A charger: 100 A breaker and 3 AWG copper for runs up to 150 ft. That is copper THHN in conduit on 75 °C terminals (NEC Table 310.16, 2023 edition), with voltage drop within 3 % on 240 V. The breaker is 125 % of the charger current, because the NEC treats charging as a continuous load (625.41).

Breaker and wire for each charger

Charger current is what the charger draws, not the breaker. 240 V single phase, copper, 75 °C terminals, 30 °C ambient, no more than three current-carrying conductors. Short runs; see the next chart for length.

Why other sites show a different number: the wiring method. THHN in conduit on 75 °C terminals is read from the 75 °C column, where 6 AWG copper is 65 A. NM-B cable is held to the 60 °C column (NEC 334.80), where 6 AWG is 55 A: less than the 60 A a 48 A charger needs, so NM-B has to be 4 AWG. NM-B is not made above 2 AWG (the dash in the chart), so an 80 A charger is wired with THHN in conduit. The ground is the minimum of NEC Table 250.122 for the breaker.

Worked example: 48 A charger, 200 ft from the panel, copper THHN in conduit

  1. Charging is a continuous load: 48 A × 125 % = 60 A, so the breaker is 60 A.
  2. By ampacity (75 °C column, NEC Table 310.16): 6 AWG copper, 65 A.
  3. Voltage drop of 6 AWG at 200 ft and 48 A: 3.95 % of 240 V. Over 3 %.
  4. Next size up, 4 AWG: 2.48 %. Under 3 %.
  5. Ground: Table 250.122 gives 10 AWG copper for a 60 A breaker; with the wire upsized from 6 AWG to 4 AWG, 250.122(B) increases it to 6 AWG.

Result: 4 AWG copper with a 6 AWG ground on a 60 A breaker.

Questions

What size wire for a 48 amp EV charger?

6 AWG copper THHN in conduit on a 60 A breaker, for runs up to 150 ft. In NM-B cable it has to be 4 AWG copper.

What size breaker for a 40 amp EV charger?

50 A: the charger is a continuous load, so the breaker is at least 125 % of 40 A. The wire is 8 AWG copper THHN in conduit, or 6 AWG copper NM-B.

What size wire for a 50 amp EV charger circuit?

A 50 A circuit carries a charger set to 40 A. It needs 8 AWG copper THHN in conduit or 6 AWG copper NM-B cable, larger on long runs: see the chart.

Can I use 6 AWG NM-B (Romex) for a 48 amp charger?

No. NM-B is limited to the 60 °C column, where 6 AWG copper is 55 A, and a 48 A charger needs a 60 A circuit. Use 4 AWG NM-B, or 6 AWG THHN in conduit, or set the charger to 40 A on a 50 A breaker.

Why 125 %?

An EV charger can run at full current for hours, so the NEC treats it as a continuous load: the breaker is rated at least 125 % of the charger's maximum current (NEC 625.41), and the conductors are sized for the same 125 % (210.19(A)(1)).

Does a longer run change the breaker?

No. The breaker follows the charger. A longer run only makes the wire larger to keep the voltage drop within 3 %.

Can my panel take the charger?

That is a separate question from the wire size: the existing service has to carry the new load. NEC 220.83 and 220.87 are the two ways to check an existing house. The EV Charger & Heat Pump Check in the Electrician Calc app (Pro) does both.

How are these sizes worked out?

The breaker is the next standard size at or above 125 % of the charger current. The wire is the smallest copper conductor whose ampacity in the 75 °C column of NEC Table 310.16 covers that, then upsized until the voltage drop at the charger current is 3 % or less on 240 V (K = 12.96 Ω·cmil/ft). These are the formulas of the wire size calculator on this site and of the Electrician Calc app.
