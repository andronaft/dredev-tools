Superheat & Subcooling Calculator

Pick one of 22 refrigerants, enter gauge pressure and line temperature, and get saturation, superheat or subcooling from the published PT chart.

Quick answer: superheat = suction line temperature − saturation temperature at the suction pressure; subcooling = saturation temperature at the liquid pressure − liquid line temperature. With R-410A, 119 psig is 40.1 °F saturated, so a 52 °F suction line has 11.9 °F of superheat.

Questions

How do you calculate superheat?

Read the suction (low-side) pressure and convert it to the saturation temperature with the refrigerant's PT chart. Measure the suction line temperature near the service valve. Superheat = suction line temperature − saturation temperature. For blends with glide such as R-454B or R-407C, use the dew point (vapor) column.

How do you calculate subcooling?

Read the liquid (high-side) pressure and convert it to the saturation temperature. Measure the liquid line temperature near the service valve. Subcooling = saturation temperature − liquid line temperature. For blends with glide, use the bubble point (liquid) column.

Should I charge by superheat or subcooling?

Fixed-orifice (piston) systems are charged by superheat against a target from the outdoor and indoor conditions (target superheat calculator). TXV and EEV systems are charged by subcooling against the target on the outdoor unit's nameplate or the manufacturer's charging chart. Always follow the manufacturer's instructions for the specific unit.

Which refrigerants does the calculator cover?

22 refrigerants: R-410A, R-454B, R-32, R-22, R-134a, R-404A, R-407C, R-407A, R-448A, R-449A, R-452A, R-513A, R-1234yf, R-507A, R-427A, R-408A, R-409A, R-290, R-600a, R-744, R-717, R-12. All of them are free to use here.

Can I open the calculator with a refrigerant already selected?

Yes. Add the refrigerant after a # sign at the end of the address, for example https://zhukovskyi.space/calculators/hvac/superheat-subcooling-calculator.html#r-454b. The links on the PT chart pages open it that way.

Is R-454B a drop-in for R-410A?

No. R-454B is an A2L (mildly flammable) refrigerant for equipment designed for it, with its own pressures, glide and safety requirements. Use the R-454B chart, not the R-410A one, and follow the A2L handling rules.

Where does this PT data come from?

From published pressure–temperature tables, one row every 5 °F: Arkema Forane® Pressure Temperature Chart (PPC/SDC 05-2025) (R-410A, R-454B, R-32, R-22, R-134a, R-404A, R-407C, R-448A, R-449A, R-452A, R-513A); Arkema Forane® Pressure-Temperature Wall Chart, Americas (2019) (R-407A, R-507A, R-427A, R-408A, R-409A); Chemours, Thermodynamic Properties of Opteon™ YF, Engineering (I/P) Units (R-1234yf); NIST Chemistry WebBook, SRD 69, saturation properties (R-290, R-600a, R-744, R-717, R-12). Between the printed points the calculator interpolates along the vapor-pressure curve. It is a reference tool: verify against the equipment manufacturer's data.
