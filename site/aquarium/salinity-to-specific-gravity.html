Salinity to specific gravity

Salinity in ppt to specific gravity and back, on the reference scale of your refractometer or hydrometer, with the density and the salt per litre.

Quick answer: 35 ppt (natural seawater) is SG 1.0264 on the 25/25 °C (77 °F) scale of hobby refractometers, 1.0269 on the 60/60 °F hydrometer scale. A reading of 1.025 at 77 °F is 33.2 ppt and 1.026 is 34.5 ppt. Values from the UNESCO seawater equation of state, EOS-80.

The same reading means more salt on a 25 °C scale than on a 60 °F one. Most hobby refractometers are 25/25 °C; check the manual.

Worked example: natural seawater, 35 ppt

  1. Density at 25 °C from EOS-80: 1023.34 kg/m³; pure water at 25 °C: 997.05 kg/m³.
  2. Specific gravity 25/25 °C: 1023.34 ÷ 997.05 = 1.0264.
  3. On a 60/60 °F hydrometer scale the same water is 1.0269; on 20/20 °C it is 1.0266.
  4. Dissolved salts: 35 g/kg × 1.0233 kg/L = 35.82 g per litre, 136 g per US gallon.

Result: 35 ppt reads 1.0264 on a 77 °F refractometer. A reading of 1.025 is 33.2 ppt.

Questions

What specific gravity is 35 ppt?

1.0264 on a 25/25 °C (77 °F) scale, the one most hobby refractometers use; 1.0266 on 20/20 °C and 1.0269 on 60/60 °F.

What salinity is SG 1.025?

33.2 ppt if your instrument reads on the 25/25 °C scale, 32.6 ppt on a 60/60 °F scale. Find the scale in the refractometer's manual.

Why are there different specific gravity scales?

Specific gravity compares the water with pure water at the same temperature, and both expand as they warm, by different amounts. So the number depends on the reference temperature: 60 °F for many older hydrometers, 77 °F (25 °C) for most refractometers sold for reef tanks.

Is ppt the same as PSU?

Practically, yes. Oceanographers measure practical salinity (PSU, no unit) by conductivity; for natural seawater it equals grams of salts per kilogram of water, parts per thousand, to within about half a percent.

How much salt do I need per gallon?

Natural seawater at 35 ppt holds about 35.8 g of dissolved salts per litre, 136 g per US gallon. Commercial salt mixes need more, because they hold water, and differ by brand: start with the package's dose and check with a refractometer.

Can I use this to correct a hydrometer read at tank temperature?

No. A hydrometer's own glass or plastic expands with temperature too, so the correction depends on the instrument. Use the maker's correction chart, or a refractometer with automatic temperature compensation.
