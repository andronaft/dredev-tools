Wire size by amps and distance

The smallest conductor that carries the load and keeps voltage drop within 3 %, for 15 to 100 A over 25 to 300 ft, at 120 V and 240 V.

Quick answer: copper, 3 % voltage drop or less. On 120 V, 20 A needs 12 AWG up to 25 ft, 10 AWG up to 50 ft, 8 AWG up to 100 ft. On 240 V, 30 A needs 10 AWG up to 75 ft, 8 AWG up to 150 ft, 6 AWG up to 200 ft, and 50 A needs 8 AWG up to 75 ft, 6 AWG up to 100 ft, 4 AWG up to 200 ft.

Checked 12 November 2026 by Andrii Zhukovskyi. Ampacities from NFPA 70, National Electrical Code, 2023 edition, Table 310.16; sizes computed with the same formulas as the wire size calculator.

For continuous loads (3 hours or more, such as EV chargers) size for 125 % of the load first. NM-B cable uses the 60 °C column and is often one size larger at short lengths. “> 500” means even 500 kcmil is over 3 %: use parallel runs or a higher voltage.

Worked example: 30 A at 240 V, 150 ft one way, copper

  1. By ampacity (75 °C column, NEC Table 310.16): 10 AWG copper, 35 A, on a 30 A breaker.
  2. Voltage drop of 10 AWG at 150 ft and 30 A: 11.24 V, which is 4.68 % of 240 V. Over 3 %.
  3. Next size up, 8 AWG: 7.07 V, or 2.94 %. Under 3 %.

Result: 8 AWG copper for 30 A at 150 ft on 240 V.

Questions

What size wire for 20 amps at 100 feet?

8 AWG copper on 120 V single phase: the drop at full load is 2.62 %, within 3 %. By ampacity alone it would be 12 AWG; the length is what pushes it up.

What size wire for 30 amps at 100 feet?

8 AWG copper on 240 V single phase: the drop at full load is 1.96 %, within 3 %. By ampacity alone it would be 10 AWG; the length is what pushes it up.

What size wire for 50 amps at 100 feet?

6 AWG copper on 240 V single phase: the drop at full load is 2.06 %, within 3 %. By ampacity alone it would be 8 AWG; the length is what pushes it up.

What size wire for 100 amps at 200 feet?

1 AWG copper on 240 V single phase: the drop at full load is 2.58 %, within 3 %. By ampacity alone it would be 3 AWG; the length is what pushes it up.

Is the distance one way or the total wire length?

One way: the distance from the panel to the load. The voltage drop formula already counts the current going out and coming back.

Does a longer run change the breaker size?

No. The breaker follows the load. A longer run only makes the wire larger to keep the voltage drop down. When you upsize the ungrounded conductors, NEC 250.122(B) has the equipment grounding conductor upsized in proportion.

How are these sizes worked out?

First the smallest conductor whose ampacity in the 75 °C column of NEC Table 310.16 covers the load and its breaker, with the 240.4(D) limits for small conductors. Then the size goes up until the voltage drop at full load is 3 % or less, using copper or aluminum resistance at operating temperature (K = 12.96 Ω·cmil/ft for copper, 21.3 for aluminum).
