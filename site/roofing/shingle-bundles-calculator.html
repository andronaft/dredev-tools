Shingle bundles and squares calculator

Roofing squares and bundles of shingles from the footprint and the pitch, with waste for gable, hip and cut-up roofs.

Quick answer: one roofing square is 100 sq ft and takes 3 bundles of standard shingles. A 2,000 sq ft roof surface is 20 squares, 60 bundles plus waste. A 2,000 sq ft footprint at 6/12 is 22.4 squares, about 74 bundles with 10 % waste.

Worked example: how many squares in a 2,000 sq ft roof

  1. If 2,000 sq ft is the measured roof surface: 2,000 ÷ 100 = 20 squares, 60 bundles before waste, 66 with 10 %.
  2. If 2,000 sq ft is the footprint under a 6/12 roof: 2,000 × 1.118 = 2,236 sq ft, or 22.4 squares.
  3. Bundles with 10 % waste: 2,236 × 1.10 ÷ 33.3 sq ft per bundle = 74.

Result: 20 squares for 2,000 sq ft of roof surface; 22.4 squares and 74 bundles for a 2,000 sq ft footprint at 6/12.

Questions

How many bundles of shingles are in a square?

Three, for standard three-tab and laminated architectural shingles: each bundle covers about 33.3 sq ft. Coverage per bundle is printed on the wrapper, and some heavier products are packed differently.

How many squares is a 2,000 sq ft roof?

20 squares if 2,000 sq ft is the roof surface. If it is the footprint of the house, the roof is larger: 21.1 squares at 4/12, 22.4 at 6/12 and 24.0 at 8/12, before overhangs you have not counted.

How many bundles do I need for 1,500 sq ft?

45 bundles for 15 squares with no waste, 50 with 10 % and 52 with 15 %.

How much waste should I add?

RoofCalc starts from 10 % for a simple gable, 15 % for a hip roof and 20 % for a cut-up roof with valleys and dormers. Manufacturers do not publish one figure, so adjust it to the roof and your own experience.

Does this include starter and ridge cap?

No. Starter strip, hip and ridge cap, underlayment, drip edge and nails are counted separately from the field shingles. The RoofCalc app works them out from the same measurements.
