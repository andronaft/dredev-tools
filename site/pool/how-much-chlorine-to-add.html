How much chlorine to add to a pool

The amount of liquid chlorine, bleach, cal-hypo, dichlor or trichlor that raises free chlorine by the ppm you need, for your pool's volume.

Quick answer: to raise free chlorine by 1 ppm in 10,000 US gallons, add about 12.8 fl oz of 10 % liquid chlorine, 2.1 oz of 65 % cal-hypo or 1.5 oz of 90 % trichlor. Scale by your pool's gallons and the ppm you need, and follow the product label.

Before you add chlorine

  • The product label comes first. Read it and follow its directions and dose limits, even where this page says something different.
  • Never mix pool chemicals with each other, and never mix different kinds of chlorine. Mixing can release toxic gas or start a fire. Use a clean, dry scoop for each product.
  • Wear goggles and gloves, work outdoors or with good ventilation, and keep children and pets away.
  • Add one chemical at a time to the pool water with the pump running. Never add water to a chemical.
  • Store chemicals in their original labelled containers, dry, closed and apart from each other.
  • Add in portions, let the water circulate, test again, and wait until the chlorine is back in its normal range before anyone swims.

More on storing and handling pool chemicals: CDC, pool chemical safety (read 12 November 2026). If someone breathes fumes or gets chemical in the eyes, call emergency services or poison control.

Worked example: 15,000 gallons, free chlorine from 2 to 5 ppm

  1. Rise needed: 5 − 2 = 3 ppm.
  2. 10 % liquid chlorine: 12.8 fl oz raises 10,000 gallons by 1 ppm.
  3. For 15,000 gallons and 3 ppm: 12.8 × 1.5 × 3 = 58 fl oz, about 0.45 gallon.
  4. The same rise with 65 % cal-hypo: 9.2 oz, which also adds 2.1 ppm of calcium hardness.

Result: about 58 fl oz of 10 % liquid chlorine, added in portions with the pump running.

Questions

How much liquid chlorine do I add per 10,000 gallons?

About 12.8 fl oz of 10 % liquid chlorine, or 10.2 fl oz of 12.5 %, for each 1 ppm of free chlorine. A 3 ppm rise takes 38 fl oz of 10 %.

How much liquid chlorine for a 20,000 gallon pool?

25.6 fl oz of 10 % liquid chlorine for each 1 ppm, so 77 fl oz (0.60 gal) for a 3 ppm rise. One gallon of 10 % adds about 5.0 ppm there. Other volumes are in the tables, and each one loads into the calculator.

How much chlorine per gallon of pool water?

Per 1,000 gallons, 1 ppm takes about 1.28 fl oz of 10 % liquid chlorine or 0.21 oz of 65 % cal-hypo. Work from the ppm you need, not from a fixed amount per gallon.

What is the difference between liquid chlorine, cal-hypo and trichlor?

All add free chlorine. Liquid chlorine and bleach add only salt. Cal-hypo also adds calcium: about 7 ppm of hardness for every 10 ppm of chlorine. Trichlor and dichlor also add stabilizer: about 6 and 9 ppm of CYA per 10 ppm of chlorine, which builds up over a season.

How much free chlorine should my pool have?

It depends on the stabilizer (CYA) level: Trouble Free Pool's chart gives a target of 4–6 ppm at 30 ppm CYA and 5–7 ppm at 40 ppm for pools without a salt cell. Test first, then dose the difference.

Can I mix two kinds of chlorine to save time?

No. Never mix pool chemicals, and never put one kind of chlorine into a feeder, bucket or scoop that held another: some combinations react violently. Add each product to the pool water separately.

How long after adding chlorine can you swim?

Follow the product label. As a rule, let the pump circulate the water, test again, and swim only when the free chlorine is back within the range for your pool.
