Conduit fill chart: THHN in EMT and PVC Schedule 40

How many THHN or THWN-2 conductors, 14 AWG to 1/0, fit in ½ to 2 in. EMT and PVC Schedule 40 conduit, with the 40 % rule and the areas behind every count.

Quick answer: at 40 % fill, ½ in. EMT holds 12 conductors of 14 AWG THHN, 9 of 12 AWG or 5 of 10 AWG. ¾ in. EMT holds 21, 16 or 10, and 1 in. EMT 35, 25 or 16 (NEC Chapter 9, Tables 1, 4 and 5, 2023 edition). Counts are rounded down, so some are one lower than Annex C.

Worked example: 12 AWG THHN in ¾ in. EMT

  1. Total area of ¾ in. EMT (Chapter 9, Table 4): 0.533 in².
  2. More than two conductors may fill 40 % (Table 1): 0.533 × 0.40 = 0.2132 in².
  3. One 12 AWG THHN is 0.0133 in² with its insulation (Table 5): 0.2132 ÷ 0.0133 = 16.03.

Result: 16 conductors.

Worked example: three 10 AWG and a 12 AWG ground in ½ in. EMT (mixed sizes)

  1. Add the areas: 3 × 0.0211 + 0.0133 = 0.0766 in².
  2. Divide by the area of ½ in. EMT: 0.0766 ÷ 0.304 = 25.2 %.
  3. Four conductors, so the limit is 40 %. The ground counts like any other conductor.

Result: 25.2 % fill, within the 40 % limit.

Questions

How many 12 AWG wires fit in 1/2 inch EMT?

9 THHN conductors at the 40 % limit. PVC Schedule 40 of the same trade size is slightly smaller inside and holds 8.

How many 12 AWG wires fit in 3/4 inch conduit?

16 THHN conductors in EMT and 15 in PVC Schedule 40. With more than three of them carrying current the ampacity has to be reduced: see how many 12 AWG wires in 3/4 EMT.

What is the 40 % rule?

NEC Chapter 9, Table 1: conductors may fill 53 % of a raceway's cross-section when there is one, 31 % when there are two and 40 % when there are more than two. Fill is the total area of the conductors with their insulation (Table 5) divided by the internal area of the raceway (Table 4).

Do ground wires count toward conduit fill?

Yes. Every conductor in the raceway counts, equipment grounding conductors included (Note 3 to the Chapter 9 tables).

Why does this chart show one less than my code book in some cells?

Annex C of the NEC rounds up when the calculation ends in .8 or more, as Note 7 to the Chapter 9 tables permits for conductors of one size. This chart always rounds down, as the Electrician Calc app does, so the marked cells are one lower. Rounding down never overfills the raceway.

Does fill decide how many circuits I can run in one conduit?

Only partly. With more than three current-carrying conductors in a raceway, NEC 310.15(C)(1) reduces their ampacity, and that limit is often reached before the fill limit.

Where do these numbers come from?

NFPA 70, National Electrical Code, 2023 edition: Chapter 9, Table 1 (fill limits), Table 4 (raceway areas) and Table 5 (THHN and THWN-2 areas), as held in the Electrician Calc app. Each count is the largest number of conductors whose total area stays within the limit.
