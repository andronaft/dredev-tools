NEC edition by state: which electrical code each state uses

The National Electrical Code edition in force in each of the 50 states and the District of Columbia, the day it took effect and what is being adopted next.

Quick answer: NEC 2023 is the most common statewide edition, in force in 18 states. The others: NEC 2026 in 6, NEC 2020 in 17, NEC 2017 in 3, NEC 2014 in 2. NEC 2026 is in force in Colorado, Maine, Massachusetts, Minnesota, North Dakota and Wyoming. Arizona, Illinois, Kansas, Mississippi and Missouri have no statewide edition that covers houses: there the city or county decides. Counts include the District of Columbia.

States where a newer edition is on the way

Arkansas, Connecticut, Florida, Indiana, Iowa, Kentucky, Maryland, Michigan, Montana, Nebraska, Oregon, South Carolina, Vermont, Virginia and Washington. The note in each row says which edition and, where the state has set one, the date.

Why the edition matters

An inspector checks the work against the edition the jurisdiction adopted, not the newest one. Article numbers and requirements move between editions, so a rule quoted from NEC 2023 may read differently, or sit in another section, where NEC 2017 or 2020 is still in force. Before a job in another state, look up its edition first.

Questions

Which states use the 2026 NEC?

Colorado, Maine, Massachusetts, Minnesota, North Dakota and Wyoming, as of 17 November 2026. NFPA's own table is dated 3 August 2026, so a state that switched after that day may still show its older edition here.

Which states have no statewide electrical code?

Arizona, Illinois, Kansas, Mississippi and Missouri. In Arizona, Mississippi and Missouri the NEC is adopted city by city or county by county. Illinois and Kansas have a state rule based on NEC 2008, but it does not cover houses in places with their own code, so the local edition is the one that counts.

Which NEC edition do most states use?

NEC 2023: 18 states enforce it statewide. NEC 2026: 6. NEC 2020: 17. NEC 2017: 3. NEC 2014: 2.

Is the NEC a federal law?

No. The National Electrical Code is a model code published by NFPA as NFPA 70. It becomes law only where a state, county or city adopts an edition, often with its own amendments.

How often is the NEC updated?

Every three years: 2017, 2020, 2023, 2026. States adopt each edition on their own schedule, which is why several editions are in force at the same time.

How do I find the edition my city enforces?

Ask the building department that issues the electrical permit, or read its permit page. A city may amend the state's edition or enforce a newer one, and the inspector's edition is the one your work is checked against.

Which edition do the calculators on this site use?

NFPA 70, National Electrical Code, 2023 edition: Table 310.16 for ampacity, Table 250.122 for grounding and Chapter 9 for conduit fill. If your state is on another edition, check the article numbers against that edition.
