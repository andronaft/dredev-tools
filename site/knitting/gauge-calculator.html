Knitting gauge calculator

From the stitches and rows you count in a swatch to the stitches to cast on and the rows to work, for any finished size.

Quick answer: stitches to cast on = stitches per 4 in ÷ 4 × the width in inches. At 18 stitches per 4 in, a 20 in piece needs 90 stitches; at 14 it needs 70. Measure the gauge on a washed swatch, away from the edges.

Worked example: a 20 in wide blanket panel

  1. You count 18 stitches and 24 rows over a 4 in square of your swatch.
  2. Per inch: 18 ÷ 4 = 4.5 stitches. Cast on 4.5 × 20 = 90 stitches.
  3. Rows: 24 ÷ 4 = 6 per inch. For 60 in of length, 6 × 60 = 360 rows.

Result: cast on 90 stitches and work 360 rows.

Questions

How do I measure knitting gauge?

Knit a swatch at least 5 in square in the pattern stitch, wash and dry it the way you will treat the finished piece, lay it flat and count the stitches and rows over 4 in in the middle, away from the edges.

How many stitches do I cast on for a 20 inch width?

80 stitches at 16 per 4 in, 90 at 18 and 100 at 20. Divide your gauge by 4 and multiply by the width.

My gauge does not match the pattern. What now?

Change the needle size and swatch again: a larger needle gives fewer stitches per inch. If you like the fabric as it is, recalculate the cast-on from your own gauge with this calculator and expect to use a different amount of yarn.

Does row gauge matter?

For pieces worked to a length ("knit until 10 in"), hardly. For shaping counted in rows, such as sleeve caps, raglans and colourwork charts, it does.

Does it work for crochet?

Yes. Count stitches and rows in a crochet swatch the same way: the arithmetic is identical. The yarn weight table above is for knitting gauge.
