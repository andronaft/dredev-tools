What is particular about R-32

One substance, no glide

R-32 is not a blend. At a given pressure it boils and condenses at a single temperature, so its chart has one pressure column and there is no bubble or dew decision to make. Whatever the calculator shows as the saturation temperature is used for superheat and for subcooling alike.

Close to R-410A, but not the same chart

R-32 runs slightly above R-410A: 121.0 psig against 118.8 psig at 40 °F, and 349.7 psig against 341.9 psig as 105 °F liquid. The gap looks small on a gauge and still moves the result: 375 psig on the liquid line is 110.0 °F for R-32 and 111.7 °F on the R-410A chart, a 1.7 °F error in subcooling. See R-32 vs R-410A.

R-32 and R-454B

Arkema lists both R-32 and R-454B as R-410A replacements for new equipment (Arkema, 2025 Forane® Technical Digest). R-454B is itself mostly R-32: R-32 / R-1234yf, 68.9 / 31.1 % by weight (Chemours, Opteon™ XL41 (R-454B) product information (C-11001, 7/24)). They are still two refrigerants. R-454B has glide and lower pressures, 107.0 psig as 40 °F vapor against 121.0 psig, and a unit is charged with the one named on its data plate. See R-454B vs R-32.

A2L class and GWP

R-32 is in safety class A2L (lower toxicity, lower flammability) (ASHRAE Standard 34 class as listed by US EPA SNAP, Substitutes in Residential and Light Commercial Air Conditioning and Heat Pumps (page updated 19 March 2026)). Chemours states that A2L refrigerants are for new equipment only, not for retrofits (Chemours, Opteon™ XL41 (R-454B) Pressure-Temperature Guide for A/C), so R-32 does not go into an R-410A system. EPA gives it a 100-year GWP of 675 (US EPA, Technology Transitions GWP Reference Table (page updated 2 April 2026)), against 2,088 for R-410A. Arkema names POE as the lubricant.

R-32 worked examples and pressures

The readings below are a comfort-cooling example. They show the arithmetic; they are not charging targets.

Worked example: R-32 superheat

  1. Suction (low-side) gauge pressure: 121 psig.
  2. Saturation temperature at 121 psig in the chart: 40.0 °F.
  3. Suction line temperature near the service valve: 52 °F.
  4. Superheat = 52 − 40.0 = 12.0 °F.

Result: 12.0 °F of superheat.

Worked example: R-32 subcooling

  1. Liquid (high-side) gauge pressure: 375 psig.
  2. Saturation temperature at 375 psig in the chart: 110.0 °F.
  3. Liquid line temperature near the service valve: 100 °F.
  4. Subcooling = 110.0 − 100 = 10.0 °F.

Result: 10.0 °F of subcooling.

What should R-32 superheat and subcooling be?

There is no single correct number for a refrigerant. The target belongs to the equipment: a fixed-orifice (piston) system is charged to a target superheat from the manufacturer's charging table (target superheat calculator), and a TXV or EEV system to the target subcooling on the unit's data plate. Always use the manufacturer's numbers for the unit in front of you.

Questions

Does R-32 have temperature glide?

No. R-32 is a single substance: one saturation temperature at each pressure, one column on the chart.

Is the R-32 PT chart the same as the R-410A chart?

No, although they are close. At 40 °F R-32 is at 121.0 psig and R-410A at 118.8 psig; at 375 psig the two charts are 1.7 °F apart.

Can R-32 be used in an R-410A system?

No. R-32 is an A2L refrigerant, and Chemours states that A2L refrigerants are for new equipment only, not for retrofits.

What is the GWP of R-32?

675 per US EPA (IPCC 2007 (AR4)), against 2,088 for R-410A.

Where does the data come from?

Arkema Forane® Pressure Temperature Chart (PPC/SDC 05-2025). Between the printed 5 °F points the calculator interpolates along the vapor-pressure curve.
