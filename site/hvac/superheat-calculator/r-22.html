What is particular about R-22

No longer made, still in service

R-22 is an HCFC with an ozone depletion potential of 0.055 (US EPA, Ozone-Depleting Substances (page updated 9 April 2026)). EPA's guidance for homeowners says that no new R-22 has been made or imported in the United States since 1 January 2020, and that existing R-22 systems can still be used and serviced, with reclaimed R-22 and R-22 made before 2020 (US EPA, Homeowners and Consumers: Frequently Asked Questions on the HCFC-22 phaseout (page updated 1 April 2026, read 13 November 2026)).

Low pressures next to R-410A

R-22 reads 68.6 psig at 40 °F and 210.8 psig as 105 °F liquid, where R-410A reads 118.8 psig and 341.9 psig. Put a 69 psig R-22 suction reading on an R-410A chart and it shows 14.2 °F instead of 40.3 °F.

After a retrofit the chart changes

Arkema lists R-407C as a replacement and retrofit refrigerant for R-22, and R-427A as an R-22 retrofit (Arkema, 2025 Forane® Technical Digest). Both are blends with two columns. At 69 psig, where R-22 has a single 40.3 °F, R-407C has a dew point of 43.9 °F and a bubble point of 32.9 °F, and R-427A 45.3 °F and 35.1 °F. Once a system has been converted, R-22 numbers no longer describe it: open the calculator with R-407C or R-427A, whichever is on the retrofit label. See also R-407C vs R-22.

Oil, class and metering device

Arkema lists mineral oil, alkylbenzene or POE as lubricants for R-22 and says POE must be used with R-407C (Arkema, 2025 Forane® Technical Digest). R-22 is in safety class A1 (lower toxicity, no flame propagation) (ASHRAE Standard 34 class as listed by US EPA SNAP, Substitutes in Refrigerated Food Processing and Dispensing Equipment (page updated 20 March 2026)), with a 100-year GWP of 1,810 per US EPA (US EPA, Ozone-Depleting Substances (page updated 9 April 2026)). An R-22 unit with a fixed orifice (piston) is charged to a target superheat: see the target superheat calculator.

R-22 worked examples and pressures

The readings below are a comfort-cooling example. They show the arithmetic; they are not charging targets.

Worked example: R-22 superheat

  1. Suction (low-side) gauge pressure: 69 psig.
  2. Saturation temperature at 69 psig in the chart: 40.3 °F.
  3. Suction line temperature near the service valve: 52 °F.
  4. Superheat = 52 − 40.3 = 11.7 °F.

Result: 11.7 °F of superheat.

Worked example: R-22 subcooling

  1. Liquid (high-side) gauge pressure: 226 psig.
  2. Saturation temperature at 226 psig in the chart: 109.9 °F.
  3. Liquid line temperature near the service valve: 100 °F.
  4. Subcooling = 109.9 − 100 = 9.9 °F.

Result: 9.9 °F of subcooling.

What should R-22 superheat and subcooling be?

There is no single correct number for a refrigerant. The target belongs to the equipment: a fixed-orifice (piston) system is charged to a target superheat from the manufacturer's charging table (target superheat calculator), and a TXV or EEV system to the target subcooling on the unit's data plate. Always use the manufacturer's numbers for the unit in front of you.

Questions

Is R-22 still available?

EPA's guidance for homeowners says no new R-22 has been made or imported in the United States since 1 January 2020. Existing systems can still be serviced with reclaimed R-22 and R-22 made before 2020.

Is R-22 a blend?

No. R-22 is a single substance, so its chart has one pressure column and one saturation temperature at each pressure.

Can I use R-22 pressures after converting to R-407C or R-427A?

No. At 69 psig R-22 is at 40.3 °F, while R-407C has a dew point of 43.9 °F and a bubble point of 32.9 °F, and R-427A 45.3 °F and 35.1 °F. Use the chart of the refrigerant that is in the system.

What is the ozone depletion potential of R-22?

0.055, per US EPA.

Where does the data come from?

Arkema Forane® Pressure Temperature Chart (PPC/SDC 05-2025). Between the printed 5 °F points the calculator interpolates along the vapor-pressure curve.
