Epoxy Floor Coverage Calculator

How many gallons and kits of epoxy, polyaspartic or polyurea you need, from area, thickness in mils and solids by volume.

Quick answer: one US gallon at 1 mil covers 1,604 sq ft. A 100 % solids epoxy at 10 mils covers about 160 sq ft per gallon; a 50 % solids product at the same dry thickness covers 80 sq ft. A 480 sq ft two-car garage at 10 mils needs about 3.3 gallons with 10 % waste.

Worked example: two-car garage, 480 sq ft at 10 mils

  1. Spread rate of a 100 % solids epoxy at 10 mils: 1,604 ÷ 10 = 160 sq ft per gallon.
  2. Material: 480 sq ft ÷ 160 = 2.99 gal, plus 10 % waste = 3.29 gal.
  3. Kits of 1.5 gal: 3.29 ÷ 1.5 = 2.19, round up to 3.

Result: 3.3 gallons, order 3 kits of 1.5 gal.

Questions

How much epoxy for a 2 car garage?

About 400–500 sq ft. At 10 mils with a 100 % solids epoxy and 10 % waste that is 3.3 gallons for 480 sq ft. A typical flake system adds a primer coat and a clear topcoat, each sized the same way.

How much epoxy do I need per square foot?

One US gallon spread 1 mil thick covers 1,604 sq ft. Divide by the thickness in mils and multiply by the solids by volume: a 100 % solids epoxy at 10 mils covers about 160 sq ft per gallon, a 50 % solids product at the same dry thickness covers half that.

What does solids by volume mean?

It is the share of the liquid that stays on the floor after the solvent or water evaporates. You will find it on the product's technical data sheet (TDS). Thickness here is the dry film thickness (DFT).

How much waste should I allow?

Installers commonly add 5–15 % for porous concrete, the roller and the bucket. Profiled or very porous slabs take more.

How many kits should I order?

Divide the gallons needed by the kit size and round up. The calculator also tells you when you are just over a kit boundary, so you can decide whether a little less waste or thickness saves one kit.
