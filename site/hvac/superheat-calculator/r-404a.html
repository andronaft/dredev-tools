What is particular about R-404A

Positive pressure on cold evaporators

R-404A keeps the suction side above atmospheric pressure down to the bottom of the chart: 0.5 psig at -50 °F, 24.6 psig at -10 °F and 56.6 psig at 20 °F. R-134a, by comparison, is in a vacuum below -15 °F.

A blend that is printed with one column

R-404A is a blend: EPA gives its GWP as calculated for the blend (US EPA, Technology Transitions GWP Reference Table (page updated 2 April 2026)). Arkema's chart prints one pressure column for it, so the calculator uses a single saturation temperature for superheat and for subcooling.

Its replacements have glide

Arkema describes R-448A and R-449A as designed to replace R-404A, and lists R-452A as a replacement for R-404A and R-507A (Arkema, 2025 Forane® Technical Digest). Those charts have two columns. At 25 psig R-404A is at -9.5 °F, while R-448A has a dew point of 0.8 °F and a bubble point of -9.9 °F, and R-449A 0.8 °F and -9.1 °F. After a conversion to R-448A, superheat taken from the R-404A chart would be 10.3 °F too high. Open the calculator with R-448A, R-449A or R-452A, and see R-448A vs R-404A and R-449A vs R-404A.

GWP, oil and neighbours

EPA gives R-404A a 100-year GWP of 3,922, the highest of the refrigerants with their own page here. Arkema lists it as the replacement for R-502 and names POE as its lubricant (Arkema, 2025 Forane® Technical Digest). It is in safety class A1 (lower toxicity, no flame propagation) (ASHRAE Standard 34 class as listed by US EPA SNAP, Substitutes in Residential and Light Commercial Air Conditioning and Heat Pumps (page updated 19 March 2026)). R-507A is a separate refrigerant with a chart of its own: at 25 psig it is at -10.6 °F.

R-404A worked examples and pressures

The readings below are a low-temperature refrigeration example. They show the arithmetic; they are not charging targets.

Worked example: R-404A superheat

  1. Suction (low-side) gauge pressure: 25 psig.
  2. Saturation temperature at 25 psig in the chart: -9.5 °F.
  3. Suction line temperature near the service valve: 2 °F.
  4. Superheat = 2 − (-9.5) = 11.5 °F.

Result: 11.5 °F of superheat.

Worked example: R-404A subcooling

  1. Liquid (high-side) gauge pressure: 254 psig.
  2. Saturation temperature at 254 psig in the chart: 104.9 °F.
  3. Liquid line temperature near the service valve: 95 °F.
  4. Subcooling = 104.9 − 95 = 9.9 °F.

Result: 9.9 °F of subcooling.

What should R-404A superheat and subcooling be?

There is no single correct number for a refrigerant. The target belongs to the equipment: a fixed-orifice (piston) system is charged to a target superheat from the manufacturer's charging table (target superheat calculator), and a TXV or EEV system to the target subcooling on the unit's data plate. Always use the manufacturer's numbers for the unit in front of you.

Questions

Does R-404A have temperature glide on this chart?

The source chart prints one pressure column for R-404A, so the calculator uses one saturation temperature for superheat and for subcooling.

What replaces R-404A?

Arkema describes R-448A and R-449A as designed to replace R-404A, and lists R-452A and R-407A as replacements for R-404A and R-507A. A conversion follows the refrigerant maker's and the equipment maker's instructions.

Can I keep using the R-404A chart after converting to R-448A or R-449A?

No. At 25 psig R-404A is at -9.5 °F, while R-448A has a dew point of 0.8 °F and a bubble point of -9.9 °F, and R-449A 0.8 °F and -9.1 °F.

Does R-404A suction pressure go into a vacuum?

Not within this chart: it reads 0.5 psig at -50 °F.

Where does the data come from?

Arkema Forane® Pressure Temperature Chart (PPC/SDC 05-2025). Between the printed 5 °F points the calculator interpolates along the vapor-pressure curve.
