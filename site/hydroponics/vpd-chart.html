VPD chart and calculator

Vapour pressure deficit for your grow room or greenhouse from air temperature and humidity, for the air and at the leaf, with the humidity that gives a target VPD.

Quick answer: VPD = e°(T) × (1 − RH ÷ 100), with e°(T) = 0.6108 × exp(17.27 T ÷ (T + 237.3)) kPa (FAO-56). At 25 °C and 60 % RH the air VPD is 1.27 kPa; with leaves 2 °C cooler it is 0.91 kPa. At 20 °C and 70 % it is 0.70 kPa, at 30 °C and 50 % 2.12 kPa.

Worked example: a grow tent at 25 °C and 60 % RH

  1. Saturation vapour pressure at the air temperature: e°(25) = 0.6108 × exp(17.27 × 25 ÷ 262.3) = 3.168 kPa.
  2. The air holds 60 % of that: 3.168 × 0.60 = 1.901 kPa.
  3. Air VPD: 3.168 − 1.901 = 1.27 kPa.
  4. The leaves read 23 °C on an infrared thermometer: e°(23) = 2.809 kPa, so leaf VPD = 2.809 − 1.901 = 0.91 kPa.
  5. For a leaf VPD of 1.2 kPa at the same temperatures the humidity would be (2.809 − 1.2) ÷ 3.168 × 100 = 51 %.

Result: 1.27 kPa in the air, 0.91 kPa at the leaf. A cooler leaf means a lower VPD.

Questions

What is VPD?

Vapour pressure deficit is how much more water vapour the air could hold before it is saturated, in kilopascals. It drives transpiration: the higher the VPD, the faster leaves lose water. Unlike relative humidity it accounts for temperature, so 60 % RH is a different climate at 20 °C than at 30 °C.

How do I calculate VPD?

Work out the saturation vapour pressure e°(T) = 0.6108 × exp(17.27 T ÷ (T + 237.3)) kPa (FAO-56, Eq. 11). Air VPD = e°(air) × (1 − RH ÷ 100). Leaf VPD = e°(leaf) − e°(air) × RH ÷ 100. At 25 °C and 60 % the air VPD is 1.27 kPa.

Why use leaf temperature?

The air spaces inside a leaf are saturated at the leaf's own temperature, which is usually a little below the air under lights. A leaf 2 °C cooler than 25 °C air at 60 % has a VPD of 0.91 kPa instead of 1.27. Measure your leaves with an infrared thermometer and enter the difference.

What VPD should I aim for?

It depends on the crop and the stage, and the published advice is guidance rather than a rule. Michigan State University's floriculture team suggests keeping VPD above about 0.5 kPa so plants transpire and foliar disease is held back, and notes that above about 1.5 kPa water loss is high. GrowLab starts from 0.4–0.8 kPa for seedlings and cuttings, 0.8–1.2 for vegetative growth and 1.0–1.5 for flowering and fruiting, as editable defaults.

What does a negative leaf VPD mean?

The leaf is at or below the dew point of the air, so water condenses on it. That happens with cool leaves in very humid air (at 25 °C and 90 % RH the dew point is 23.2 °C) and invites mould: raise the temperature or lower the humidity.

Does altitude change VPD?

No. VPD depends on temperature and humidity only; air pressure does not enter the formula.
