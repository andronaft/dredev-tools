Honey per gallon calculator

How much honey a mead needs for a target original gravity, or the gravity and potential alcohol your honey will give.

Quick answer: one pound of honey in one US gallon of must adds about 35 gravity points. For a 1.100 mead use about 2.9 lb per gallon (14.3 lb for 5 gallons); 3 lb per gallon gives about 1.105. Honey varies, so measure after mixing.

Same 35 points per pound per gallon. The gallon is the finished must, honey included: mix the honey with part of the water, then top up to the mark.

Worked example: 5 gallons of mead at 1.100

  1. Target gravity 1.100 is 100 gravity points above water.
  2. Honey: 100 points × 5 gal ÷ 35 points per lb per gal = 14.29 lb (6.48 kg).
  3. That is 2.86 lb per gallon. Fermented dry to 1.000 it would reach 13.1 % ABV.

Result: about 14.3 lb of honey, topped up with water to 5 gallons.

Questions

How much honey do I need for 1 gallon of mead?

About 2.3 lb for a lighter mead at 1.080, 2.9 lb for 1.100 and 3.4 lb for a strong one at 1.120, counting 35 gravity points per pound per gallon.

How much honey for 5 gallons of mead?

11.4 lb for 1.080, 14.3 lb for 1.100 or 17.1 lb for 1.120. Top up with water to the 5 gallon mark after the honey is in.

Is the gallon the water or the finished must?

The finished must. Honey takes up room, so the formula uses the final volume: dissolve the honey in part of the water and top up to the mark.

Why is my gravity different from the calculator?

Honey is not a standard product: its water content changes from jar to jar, and so do the points it gives. 35 points per pound per gallon is an average. Stir until the honey is fully dissolved and trust the hydrometer.

Can I add honey later to raise the alcohol?

Yes, that is step feeding. Set the starting gravity to your current reading and the calculator shows the honey for the points you want to add.
