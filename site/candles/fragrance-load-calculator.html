Fragrance load calculator

How much fragrance oil to weigh for your wax, in ounces or grams, and whether the load fits what the supplier lists for that wax.

Quick answer: fragrance = wax weight × load %. At 8 % one pound of wax takes 1.28 oz of fragrance oil, at 10 % it takes 1.6 oz. "1 oz per pound" is 6.25 %. Most waxes hold 6–12 %; check your wax's data sheet.

Worked example: 1 lb of soy wax at 8 %

  1. Fragrance: 16 oz × 8 ÷ 100 = 1.28 oz.
  2. Wax plus fragrance: 16 + 1.28 = 17.28 oz to pour.
  3. The same recipe counted against the whole candle is 7.41 %: 8 % of the wax is not 8 % of the candle.

Result: weigh out 1.28 oz (36 g) of fragrance oil for 1 lb of wax.

Questions

How do I calculate fragrance load?

Fragrance weight = wax weight × load ÷ 100, when the load is a percentage of the wax (the usual way in the US). 16 oz of wax at 8 % needs 1.28 oz of fragrance oil.

Is the load a percentage of the wax or of the whole candle?

Most suppliers mean the wax. Some recipes mean the finished candle, which is a little more oil: 10 % of the wax is 9.09 % of the candle, and 10 % of the candle is 11.11 % of the wax. Check which one your recipe uses.

How much fragrance oil per pound of wax?

1 oz per pound is 6.25 %, 1.3 oz is about 8 %, 1.6 oz is 10 % and 1.9 oz is about 12 %.

What is the maximum fragrance load?

It depends on the wax. CandleScience lists 10 % for Golden Brands 464 and 444, IGI 6006 and IGI 4630, and 12 % for IGI 4627 in container candles. More oil than the wax can hold seeps out and can burn badly, so stay within your wax maker's data sheet.

Does this work for wax melts?

The arithmetic is the same: wax weight × load. Melt waxes have their own limits, so use the figure from that wax's data sheet.
