16 × 20 concrete slab

Cubic yards, 40, 60 and 80 lb bags and rebar for a 16 by 20 foot slab, at 4, 5, 6 and 8 inches thick. Change any number in the calculator.

Quick answer: a 16 × 20 ft slab is 320 sq ft. At 4 in thick it takes 3.95 cubic yards of concrete, 4.35 with 10 % extra: order 4.5 yards of ready-mix or 196 bags of 80 lb. At 6 in thick it takes 6.52 yards with the extra.

Worked example: a 16 × 20 ft slab, 4 in thick

  1. Area: 16 × 20 = 320 sq ft. Perimeter for the forms: 2 × (16 + 20) = 72 ft.
  2. Volume: 320 sq ft × 4 in ÷ 12 = 106.7 cu ft, ÷ 27 = 3.95 cu yd.
  3. With 10 % extra for uneven ground and spillage: 4.35 cu yd. Ready-mix is ordered by the quarter yard: 4.5 cu yd.
  4. In bags: 117.3 cu ft ÷ 0.60 = 196 bags of 80 lb, or ÷ 0.45 = 261 bags of 60 lb. Over a cubic yard, so ready-mix is usually less work than 196 bags.
  5. Rebar at 16 in on centre: 13 bars of 19.5 ft and 16 bars of 15.5 ft, cut from 29 sticks of 20 ft #4, 335 lb of steel.

Result: order 4.5 cu yd of ready-mix or 196 bags of 80 lb, and 29 sticks of #4 rebar.

Questions

How many yards of concrete do I need for a 16x20 slab?

3.95 cubic yards at 4 in thick, 4.94 at 5 in and 5.93 at 6 in, before waste. With 10 % extra, order 4.5 yards for a 4 in slab and 6.75 for a 6 in slab.

How many bags of concrete for a 16x20 slab?

At 4 in thick with 10 % extra: 196 bags of 80 lb, 261 bags of 60 lb or 392 bags of 40 lb. Over a cubic yard, so ready-mix is usually less work than 196 bags.

How much rebar do I need for a 16x20 slab?

A #4 grid at 16 in on centre takes 29 sticks of 20 ft (502 ft of bar, 335 lb). At 12 in on centre it is 38 sticks, at 24 in 20. Use the spacing on your plans.

How thick should a 16x20 slab be?

That depends on what it carries and on the ground under it, so take it from your plans, your engineer or the local building department. The table shows the concrete for 4, 5, 6 and 8 in so you can price each.

How much extra concrete should I order?

Estimating guides commonly add 5–10 % for spillage and an uneven subgrade, more for complicated forms or pumping. Running short in the middle of a pour costs far more than a little left over.
