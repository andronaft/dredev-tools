How much chlorine, baking soda, stabilizer or salt to add, from your pool's volume and your test results.

Quick answer: to raise free chlorine by 1 ppm in 10,000 US gallons add about 12.8 fl oz of 10 % liquid chlorine, 21 fl oz of 6 % bleach or 2.1 oz of 65 % cal-hypo. 1.4 lb of baking soda raises total alkalinity by 10 ppm.

Worked example: 15,000 gallons, free chlorine from 2 to 5 ppm

  1. Rise needed: 5 − 2 = 3 ppm.
  2. 10 % liquid chlorine raises 10,000 gallons by 1 ppm with 12.8 fl oz.
  3. For 15,000 gallons and 3 ppm: 12.8 × 1.5 × 3 = 58 fl oz.

Result: about 58 fl oz (1.70 L) of 10 % liquid chlorine.

Questions

How much liquid chlorine to raise FC by 1 ppm?

About 12.8 fl oz of 10 % liquid chlorine per 10,000 US gallons. Bleach is weaker: 6 % bleach needs roughly 21 fl oz for the same rise.

What FC should I aim for?

It depends on your stabilizer (CYA) level: the higher the CYA, the more free chlorine you need. PoolPro uses Trouble Free Pool's FC/CYA chart for targets.

Where does the math come from?

Chlorine strength follows the same bleach density formula as TFP Pool Math; baking soda raises total alkalinity by 1 ppm per 1.68 mg per litre; stabilizer and salt are added at their label purity.

How much baking soda to raise alkalinity 10 ppm?

1.40 lb (about 0.64 kg) of baking soda per 10,000 US gallons. For 20,000 gallons, twice that.
