Bubble point vs dew point: how to read a PT chart for a blend

Blends such as R-454B, R-407C and R-448A have two pressure columns. Here is which one to use, and how big the error is if you pick the wrong one.

Quick answer: use the dew point (vapor column) for superheat and the bubble point (liquid column) for subcooling. The gap between them at one pressure is the temperature glide: about 2.3 °F for R-454B and 11.0 °F for R-407C at a 40 °F dew point.

Checked 12 November 2026 by Andrii Zhukovskyi. Glide figures and examples are computed from the PT charts on this site.

What the two columns mean

A single-component refrigerant boils at one temperature for a given pressure. A zeotropic blend does not: its components boil off at different rates, so at one pressure it boils over a range of temperatures.

  • Bubble point (saturated liquid): the temperature at which the liquid starts to boil. Chart column: liquid.
  • Dew point (saturated vapor): the temperature at which the last liquid has boiled off, or at which vapor starts to condense. Chart column: vapor.

Chemours prints the same rule in the key of its R-454B chart: saturated liquid pressure to calculate subcooling, saturated vapor pressure to calculate superheat (Chemours, Opteon™ XL41 (R-454B) Pressure-Temperature Guide for A/C).

Worked example with R-407C

Worked example: superheat from the dew column

  1. Suction pressure: 63 psig.
  2. Vapor (dew) column at 63 psig: 39.9 °F. The bubble column at the same pressure reads 28.8 °F, which is the wrong value here.
  3. Suction line temperature: 52 °F.
  4. Superheat = 52 − 39.9 = 12.1 °F. With the bubble column it would come out as 23.2 °F.

Result: 12.1 °F of superheat.

Worked example: subcooling from the bubble column

  1. Liquid pressure: 260 psig.
  2. Liquid (bubble) column at 260 psig: 109.9 °F. The dew column reads 118.5 °F.
  3. Liquid line temperature: 100 °F.
  4. Subcooling = 109.9 − 100 = 9.9 °F. With the dew column it would come out as 18.5 °F.

Result: 9.9 °F of subcooling.

Questions

Do I use bubble or dew for superheat?

Dew. Superheat is measured on the suction line, where the refrigerant is vapor, so the saturation temperature comes from the vapor (dew) column.

Do I use bubble or dew for subcooling?

Bubble. Subcooling is measured on the liquid line, so the saturation temperature comes from the liquid (bubble) column.

What is temperature glide?

The difference between the dew point and the bubble point of a blend at the same pressure. For R-407C on this site's chart it is about 11.0 °F at a 40 °F dew point.

What happens if I use the wrong column?

The result shifts by the glide. On R-407C at 63 psig the dew point is 39.9 °F and the bubble point 28.8 °F, so superheat read from the bubble column would be 11.0 °F too high.

Why do some charts have only one column?

Single-component refrigerants boil at one temperature for each pressure, and for some blends the source chart prints a single column. On this site that is R-410A, R-32, R-22, R-134a, R-404A, R-1234yf, R-507A, R-408A, R-290, R-600a, R-744, R-717, R-12.
