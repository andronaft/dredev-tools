Target Superheat Calculator (Fixed Orifice)

Target superheat for a fixed-orifice (piston) air conditioner from the outdoor temperature and the indoor wet bulb, read from a manufacturer's superheat charging table.

Quick answer: at 95 °F outdoors and a 66 °F indoor wet bulb the table gives a target superheat of 10 °F; at 85 °F outdoors and the same wet bulb, 15 °F. Hotter outdoors means a lower target, more humid return air a higher one.

Outdoor: air entering the condenser. Indoor: wet bulb of the return air entering the evaporator coil.

Worked example: 92 °F outdoors, 65 °F indoor wet bulb

  1. 92 °F lies between the 90 °F and 95 °F rows, and 65 °F between the 64 °F and 66 °F columns.
  2. The four cells around the point are 9 and 13 °F (90 °F row), 6 and 10 °F (95 °F row).
  3. Interpolating between them on both axes gives 9.8 °F.

Result: target superheat of about 9.8 °F at the low-side service port.

Questions

What is target superheat?

The superheat a fixed-orifice (piston) air conditioner should show at the suction service port when the charge is right. It changes with the load: the outdoor temperature and the wet bulb of the air entering the indoor coil.

How do I use the target?

Per the charging procedure in the source document: run the system at least 10 minutes, measure the actual superheat at the suction service port and compare it with the table. Higher than the target means the system needs refrigerant; lower means it has too much. Work out the actual superheat with the superheat calculator.

What does a dash in the table mean?

The manufacturer gives no superheat target for those conditions, so the charge must not be set by superheat there. Weigh in the charge instead, or wait for conditions inside the table.

Does this work for TXV or EEV systems?

No. An expansion valve holds superheat roughly constant, so those systems are charged by subcooling against the target on the unit's data plate.

Which wet bulb temperature do I use?

The wet bulb of the air entering the indoor (evaporator) coil, measured in the return air close to the unit. The outdoor temperature is the dry bulb of the air entering the condenser.

Is this table right for my unit?

It is one manufacturer's table, printed for Bryant 563C and 563G split systems. Other units have their own charging charts, often on the unit or in the installation instructions. Where your unit has its own chart, that chart governs.
