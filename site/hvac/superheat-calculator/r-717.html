Enter gauge pressure and line temperature for R-717. Saturation comes from the published R-717 PT chart.

Quick answer: with R-717, superheat = suction line temperature − saturation temperature at the suction pressure, and subcooling = saturation temperature at the liquid pressure − liquid line temperature. Example: 33 psig on the suction side is 19.5 °F saturated, so a 32 °F suction line has 12.5 °F of superheat.

R-717 worked examples and saturation table

The readings below are a medium-temperature refrigeration example. They show the arithmetic; they are not charging targets.

Worked example: R-717 superheat

  1. Suction (low-side) gauge pressure: 33 psig.
  2. Saturation temperature at 33 psig in the chart: 19.5 °F.
  3. Suction line temperature near the service valve: 32 °F.
  4. Superheat = 32 − 19.5 = 12.5 °F.

Result: 12.5 °F of superheat.

Worked example: R-717 subcooling

  1. Liquid (high-side) gauge pressure: 214 psig.
  2. Saturation temperature at 214 psig in the chart: 104.9 °F.
  3. Liquid line temperature near the service valve: 95 °F.
  4. Subcooling = 104.9 − 95 = 9.9 °F.

Result: 9.9 °F of subcooling.

What should R-717 superheat and subcooling be?

There is no single correct number for a refrigerant. The target belongs to the equipment: a fixed-orifice (piston) system is charged to a target superheat from the manufacturer's charging table (target superheat calculator), and a TXV or EEV system to the target subcooling on the unit's data plate. Always use the manufacturer's numbers for the unit in front of you.

It is a B2L refrigerant (toxic, mildly flammable): for trained industrial technicians only.

Questions

How do I calculate superheat for R-717?

Convert the suction pressure to the saturation temperature with the R-717 PT chart and subtract it from the suction line temperature. Example: 33 psig is 19.5 °F, and a 32 °F line gives 12.5 °F of superheat.

How do I calculate subcooling for R-717?

Convert the liquid pressure to the saturation temperature and subtract the liquid line temperature. Example: 214 psig is 104.9 °F, and a 95 °F liquid line gives 9.9 °F of subcooling.

What is the saturation temperature of R-717 at 33 psig?

19.5 °F (-6.9 °C).

What superheat or subcooling should a R-717 system have?

It depends on the equipment, not on the refrigerant alone. Use the target superheat from the manufacturer's charging table for a fixed-orifice system, or the target subcooling on the data plate for a TXV or EEV system.

Where does the data come from?

NIST Chemistry WebBook, SRD 69, saturation properties. Between the printed 5 °F points the calculator interpolates along the vapor-pressure curve.

Reference only. Charging targets and procedures come from the equipment manufacturer's data plate and installation instructions, and local codes apply. Handling refrigerants requires EPA Section 608 certification in the US.
