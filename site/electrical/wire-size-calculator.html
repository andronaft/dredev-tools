Wire size calculator (NEC)

The smallest copper or aluminum conductor for your load and breaker, by NEC Table 310.16, with an optional 3 % voltage drop check.

Quick answer: with copper THHN and 75 °C terminations, a 20 A circuit needs 12 AWG, 30 A needs 10 AWG, 40 A needs 8 AWG, 50 A needs 8 AWG and 60 A needs 6 AWG (NEC Table 310.16). NM-B (Romex) uses the 60 °C column, so it is often one size larger. Long runs may need a bigger wire to keep voltage drop under 3 %.

For continuous loads (3 hours or more, such as EV chargers and water heaters) size for 125 % of the load first. The last column is the one-way length at which the copper THHN size reaches a 3 % voltage drop at full load on 240 V single phase; on 120 V it is half that. Dwelling services and main feeders can use the smaller sizes of NEC 310.12.

Worked example: 40 A range on NM-B, 240 V, 60 ft

  1. A range is not a continuous load, so the design current is 40 A and the breaker is 40 A (no 125 % factor).
  2. NM-B is limited to the 60 °C column by NEC 334.80. 10 AWG copper is 30 A there, too small. 8 AWG copper is 40 A, enough for 40 A.
  3. Voltage drop at 60 ft one way: 2 × 40 A × 18.3 m × (0.0216 Ω·mm²/m ÷ 8.37 mm²) = 3.77 V, or 1.57 % of 240 V. Under 3 %, so no upsizing.
  4. The equipment grounding conductor for a 40 A breaker is 10 AWG copper (NEC Table 250.122); 8/3 NM-B already has one.

Result: 8 AWG copper NM-B on a 40 A breaker.

Questions

What size wire for a 30 amp breaker?

With copper THHN and 75 °C terminations, 10 AWG (35 A at 75 °C, and 240.4(D) limits 10 AWG copper to 30 A). Long runs may need a larger size for voltage drop.

Why 125 % for continuous loads?

NEC 210.19(A)(1) and 215.2 require conductors for continuous loads (3 hours or more, like EV chargers and water heaters) to carry 125 % of the continuous current.

Why does NM-B use the 60 °C column?

NEC 334.80 limits NM cable ampacity to the 60 °C column, even though the conductors are rated 90 °C.

What size wire for a 20 amp breaker?

12 AWG copper or 10 AWG aluminum with 75 °C terminations (NEC Table 310.16, 30 °C ambient, up to 3 current-carrying conductors). In NM-B (Romex), 12 AWG. At full load on 240 V, 12 AWG copper reaches a 3 % voltage drop at about 91 ft one way; longer runs need a larger size.

What size wire for a 40 amp breaker?

8 AWG copper or 8 AWG aluminum with 75 °C terminations (NEC Table 310.16, 30 °C ambient, up to 3 current-carrying conductors). In NM-B (Romex), 8 AWG. At full load on 240 V, 8 AWG copper reaches a 3 % voltage drop at about 115 ft one way; longer runs need a larger size.

What size wire for a 50 amp breaker?

8 AWG copper or 6 AWG aluminum with 75 °C terminations (NEC Table 310.16, 30 °C ambient, up to 3 current-carrying conductors). In NM-B (Romex), 6 AWG. At full load on 240 V, 8 AWG copper reaches a 3 % voltage drop at about 92 ft one way; longer runs need a larger size.

What size wire for a 60 amp breaker?

6 AWG copper or 4 AWG aluminum with 75 °C terminations (NEC Table 310.16, 30 °C ambient, up to 3 current-carrying conductors). In NM-B (Romex), 4 AWG. At full load on 240 V, 6 AWG copper reaches a 3 % voltage drop at about 121 ft one way; longer runs need a larger size.

What size wire for a 100 amp breaker?

3 AWG copper or 1 AWG aluminum with 75 °C terminations (NEC Table 310.16, 30 °C ambient, up to 3 current-carrying conductors). NM-B is not made that large: use SER or THHN in conduit. At full load on 240 V, 3 AWG copper reaches a 3 % voltage drop at about 146 ft one way; longer runs need a larger size.

What size wire for a 200 amp breaker?

3/0 AWG copper or 250 kcmil aluminum with 75 °C terminations (NEC Table 310.16, 30 °C ambient, up to 3 current-carrying conductors). NM-B is not made that large: use SER or THHN in conduit. At full load on 240 V, 3/0 AWG copper reaches a 3 % voltage drop at about 233 ft one way; longer runs need a larger size.
