How to charge an R-454B system by subcooling

The subcooling method for R-454B units with a TXV or EEV, step by step, with the manufacturer documents behind each step.

Quick answer: read the liquid-line pressure, convert it to the saturated liquid (bubble) temperature on the R-454B chart, subtract the liquid line temperature, and compare the result with the target subcooling on the unit's rating plate. Add or recover refrigerant, as liquid, until it matches.

The target is on the equipment, not on this page. Target subcooling, the allowed tolerance and the conditions for charging come from the rating plate, charging label and installation instructions of the unit you are working on. The numbers below are examples of the arithmetic only. Refrigerant work requires EPA Section 608 certification in the US, and R-454B is an A2L (mildly flammable) refrigerant with its own handling rules.

Checked 12 November 2026 by Andrii Zhukovskyi. Each step cites the manufacturer document it was read in. The example uses the R-454B PT chart on this site.

Before you start

  • Confirm the method. Carrier's instructions require the subcooling method for units with a cooling-mode TXV and send you to the rating plate for the required subcooling (Carrier PH5SAN5 single-stage heat pump with R-454B, Installation Instructions). Lennox puts the target subcooling values on the unit's charging label (Lennox ML13KC1 Installation Instructions 508732-02 (03/2026)). A fixed-orifice system is charged by superheat instead: see the target superheat calculator.
  • A2L precautions. The manufacturers' instructions call for non-sparking tools, a refrigerant detector before and during the work, no open flames or other ignition sources except while brazing, and never a flame for leak checking (Carrier PH5SAN5 single-stage heat pump with R-454B, Installation Instructions); a well-ventilated work area (Lennox ML13KC1 Installation Instructions 508732-02 (03/2026)); and service equipment rated for R-454B (York YH9/XH9 R-454B heat pump Installation Manual 6515371-UIM-B-0526). Lennox notes that R-454B cylinders have a ¼″ left-hand flare connection.
  • Conditions. Carrier calls conditions favorable for checking the charge when the outdoor temperature is between 70 °F and 100 °F and the indoor temperature between 70 °F and 80 °F; outside that range its instructions have the charge weighed in for the line length and checked again later (Carrier PH5SAN5 single-stage heat pump with R-454B, Installation Instructions). Other makers set their own limits, so read the instructions for your unit.

Steps

  1. Run the system in cooling until it is stable. Carrier specifies at least 15 minutes before checking the charge (Carrier PH5SAN5 single-stage heat pump with R-454B, Installation Instructions).
  2. Read the liquid-line (high-side) pressure at the liquid service valve.
  3. Measure the liquid line temperature close to the same valve, with the probe well insulated from the air.
  4. Convert the pressure to the saturated liquid temperature with the R-454B PT chart. R-454B is a blend, so use the liquid (bubble) column. Chemours' chart says the same: saturated liquid pressure for subcooling, saturated vapor for superheat (Chemours, Opteon™ XL41 (R-454B) Pressure-Temperature Guide for A/C).
  5. Subcooling = saturated liquid temperature − liquid line temperature. The R-454B calculator does both steps.
  6. Compare with the target on the rating plate or charging label. Carrier allows a tolerance of ±3 °F around its target (Carrier PH5SAN5 single-stage heat pump with R-454B, Installation Instructions); use the tolerance your unit's instructions give.
  7. Subcooling below the target: add refrigerant. Above the target: recover some. Charge R-454B as liquid only (Carrier, Residential Piping and Long Line Guideline for AC and heat pumps using R-454B, Lennox ML13KC1 Installation Instructions 508732-02 (03/2026), Chemours, Opteon™ XL41 (R-454B) Pressure-Temperature Guide for A/C), in small steps, and let the system settle again before you take the next reading.

Worked example

An example of the arithmetic, not a target. Suppose the rating plate of the unit asks for 12 °F of subcooling.

Worked example: R-454B subcooling

  1. Liquid pressure at the service valve: 346 psig.
  2. Liquid (bubble) column of the R-454B chart at 346 psig: 110.0 °F. (The vapor column reads 112.3 °F; using it would overstate subcooling by 2.3 °F.)
  3. Liquid line temperature: 100 °F.
  4. Subcooling = 110.0 − 100 = 10.0 °F.
  5. Against the 12 °F target that is 2.0 °F under, inside a ±3 °F tolerance, so the charge stays as it is.

Result: 10.0 °F of subcooling.

Questions

What subcooling should an R-454B system have?

The value on the outdoor unit's rating plate or charging label. It differs from model to model, so there is no number that fits every R-454B unit.

Which PT column do I use for R-454B subcooling?

The liquid (bubble) column. At 346 psig it reads 110.0 °F; the vapor (dew) column reads 112.3 °F at the same pressure.

Can I add R-454B as a vapor?

No. R-454B is a blend, and the equipment and refrigerant makers require it to leave the cylinder as liquid.

Can I top off an R-410A system with R-454B?

No. R-454B is for equipment designed for it. Chemours states that A2L refrigerants are for new equipment only, not for retrofits.

How long should the system run before I check the charge?

Follow the unit's instructions. Carrier's R-454B heat pump instructions call for at least 15 minutes of operation before checking the charge.
