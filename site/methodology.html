How these tools are made

Methodology and sources

Which published tables and editions each calculator uses, what it assumes, and where it stops. If a number here is wrong, I want to know.

How the numbers are made and checked

Every calculator on this site uses a published table or a formula that is named on its page and listed below. The browser calculators run the same formulas as the Dre Dev phone apps.

They are checked in two ways. Each app has its own tests against the published values. On this site, 31 checked examples put a row from a source next to the calculator's answer; they run through the calculator code every time the site is built, and the build stops if one is outside its tolerance.

The tools are made and checked by Andrii Zhukovskyi, the developer. "Checked" means compared with the sources below. No outside trade professional has reviewed these pages yet. The sources were last read against the examples on 12 November 2026.

The code that builds the site, the formulas and the examples are public: github.com/andronaft/dredev-tools.

Limits that apply to every tool

Reference only. A result depends on what you enter and on the published tables named on this page. Before you buy materials, size a circuit, charge a system or dose chemicals, check it against the code in force where you work, the product label and the manufacturer's instructions. These tools come with no warranty.

Found a wrong number? Send it with the page, what you entered and the source you compared with. Corrections are listed in the changelog.

Superheat & subcooling calculator and PT charts

Tables and formulas

  • Pressure–temperature rows as printed by the manufacturer, one row every 5 °F. The source of each refrigerant is listed under the table below and on its chart page.
  • Gauge pressure is turned into absolute pressure with the standard atmosphere, 14.696 psia. Between rows the calculator interpolates along the vapor-pressure curve: straight in ln(P) against 1/T.
  • For blends with glide, superheat is read from the vapor (dew) column and subcooling from the liquid (bubble) column.
  • Cross-checks done in the HVAC Charge app the data comes from: Arkema's R-454B columns agree with Chemours' 1 °F table for Opteon™ XL41 within 0.06 psi, and the interpolated values agree with CoolProp 8.0 (NIST reference equations).

Assumptions

  • Sea-level atmospheric pressure. The calculator does not correct a gauge reading for altitude.
  • Readings are taken at the service valves, as the questions on the page describe.

Limits

  • Answers only inside the printed range of the chart; outside it the page says so instead of extrapolating.
  • It reports superheat or subcooling. It does not tell you the target for a unit: that comes from the manufacturer's charging instructions.
  • Handling refrigerants requires EPA Section 608 certification in the US.

Wire size calculator

Tables and formulas

  • NEC (NFPA 70) 2023, Table 310.16: ampacities of copper and aluminum conductors in the 60, 75 and 90 °C columns, 14 AWG to 500 kcmil.
  • NEC 240.4(D) for small conductors, 334.80 for NM-B cable (60 °C column), 210.19(A)(1) and 215.2 for the 125 % continuous-load rule.
  • Standard breaker ratings from 15 A to 600 A. A conductor may sit on the next standard breaker size above its ampacity, as the code allows up to 800 A.

Assumptions

  • 30 °C ambient and no more than three current-carrying conductors: no temperature or bundling correction.
  • The lower of the insulation column and the terminal rating you pick (60 or 75 °C).
  • The optional voltage drop check uses 3 % at full load and unity power factor.

Limits

  • No derating, no motor or HVAC nameplate rules, no parallel conductors, nothing above 500 kcmil or 600 A.
  • Local amendments and the edition your jurisdiction has adopted may differ from NEC 2023.
  • It sizes the ungrounded conductors only; grounding conductors, conduit fill and box fill are not checked.

Voltage drop calculator

Tables and formulas

  • NEC sizes: conductor areas from NEC Chapter 9, Table 8, with resistivity at 20 °C (copper 0.017241, aluminum 0.028264 Ω·mm²/m) raised by 25 % for operating temperature.
  • IEC sizes: IEC 60364-5-52, Annex G, with ρ = 0.0225 Ω·mm²/m for copper and 0.036 for aluminum, and a reactance of 0.08 mΩ/m on AC.
  • The 3 % and 5 % limits are the NEC informational notes and the IEC 60364-5-52 guidance; they are recommendations.

Assumptions

  • One-way length; the formula counts the return conductor (or the three phases).
  • A balanced load and one conductor per phase.

Limits

  • It checks voltage drop only. Size the conductor for ampacity and overcurrent protection as well.
  • The NEC-mode result runs about 0.5 % above the rounded K-factor formula (K = 12.9).

AWG to mm² converter

Tables and formulas

  • NEC Chapter 9, Table 8: areas in circular mils, converted with 1 cmil = 0.000506707 mm².
  • Metric sizes are the IEC 60228 series.

Assumptions

  • Area only.

Limits

  • An equal or larger area does not prove equal ampacity: insulation, installation method and the code in force decide that.

Epoxy coverage calculator

Tables and formulas

  • One US gallon is 231 cubic inches, so it covers 1,604 sq ft at 1 mil. Gallons = area × mils ÷ (1,604 × solids by volume) × (1 + waste).

Assumptions

  • Thickness is the dry film thickness. Solids by volume comes from the product's technical data sheet.
  • Kits are rounded up to whole kits.

Limits

  • Surface profile and porosity are covered only by the waste percentage you enter.
  • It does not know a product's recoat window, pot life or minimum film thickness: read the data sheet.

Pool chemical calculator

Tables and formulas

  • Doses come from stoichiometry: ppm × litres = milligrams, converted by molar mass and label strength.
  • Liquid chlorine by trade percent; bleach by weight percent with the density formula used by Trouble Free Pool's Pool Math; baking soda as 84.007 g of NaHCO3 per 50.04 g of CaCO3.
  • Cross-checked against Pool Math's classic calculator (read 25 October 2026).

Assumptions

  • Stabilizer and salt are taken as 99 % pure.
  • The pool volume you enter is right.

Limits

  • It only raises free chlorine, total alkalinity, stabilizer and salt. It does not handle pH, calcium or CSI; the PoolPro app does.
  • Dichlor and cal-hypo also add stabilizer or calcium; the page says so but does not count them.
  • Add in portions and retest. Never mix chlorine products with each other or with acid.

Baker's percentage calculator

Tables and formulas

  • Baker's percentages as defined by King Arthur Baking and the total-formula method: every ingredient is a share of the flour weight.
  • True hydration = (water + water in the starter) ÷ (flour + flour in the starter).

Assumptions

  • The starter percentage is a share of the flour in the bowl, as home recipes write it.

Limits

  • Arithmetic on your own numbers. It ships no fermentation times, temperatures or nutrition data.

Lye calculator

Tables and formulas

  • Saponification (SAP) values from the SoapCalc oil list, snapshot of 25 October 2026, for 19 common oils.
  • NaOH SAP = KOH SAP × 39.997 ÷ 56.106 (molar masses). Lye = Σ(oil grams × SAP) × (1 − superfat) ÷ purity. Water follows from the lye concentration.
  • In the Lye Calc app the same values are checked against a second published table and against published recipes.

Assumptions

  • Sodium hydroxide only on this page; the app also does KOH and dual lye.

Limits

  • Oils vary between suppliers and harvests: treat the result as a starting point and run a new recipe through a second calculator.
  • Lye is caustic: gloves, goggles, ventilation, and always add lye to water.
