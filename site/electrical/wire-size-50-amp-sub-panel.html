What size wire for a 50 amp sub panel?

Feeder wire for 50, 60 and 100 amp subpanels from 25 to 250 ft, copper and aluminum, with the voltage drop at each length.

Quick answer: a 50 amp subpanel needs 8 AWG copper or 6 AWG aluminum feeder conductors (THHN, XHHW-2 or SER cable on 75 °C terminals; NEC Table 310.16, 2023 edition). On 240 V that keeps voltage drop within 3 % at a full 50 A up to 75 ft one way; at 100 ft use 6 AWG copper or 4 AWG aluminum. The feeder has four conductors: two hots, a neutral and a ground.

The breaker in the main panel is the feeder rating: 50, 60 or 100 A. A longer run does not change it; it only makes the wire larger. If the subpanel's real load is lower than the breaker, the drop is lower in the same proportion. Why other sites show a different number: NM-B (Romex) cable is held to the 60 °C column by NEC 334.80, where 8 AWG copper is only 40 A. On NM-B a 50 A feeder needs 6 AWG copper and a 60 A feeder 4 AWG.

Four conductors, and the size of the ground

A feeder to a subpanel has four conductors: two ungrounded (hot) conductors, a neutral and an equipment grounding conductor. In the subpanel the neutral bar is isolated from the enclosure and from the ground bar, because the neutral is bonded to ground only at the service (NEC 250.24(B)). A subpanel in a detached building also needs its own grounding electrode, connected to the ground bar (NEC 250.32). The charts above size the two hots; run the neutral the same size unless a load calculation allows a smaller one (NEC 220.61).

Worked example: 60 A subpanel in a detached garage, 150 ft from the main panel, copper

  1. By ampacity (75 °C column, NEC Table 310.16): 6 AWG copper, 65 A, on a 60 A breaker.
  2. Voltage drop of 6 AWG at 150 ft and a full 60 A: 8.89 V, which is 3.71 % of 240 V. Over 3 %.
  3. Next size up, 4 AWG: 5.59 V, or 2.33 %. Under 3 %.
  4. Ground: Table 250.122 gives 10 AWG copper for a 60 A breaker. Because the hots went up from 6 AWG to 4 AWG, 250.122(B) increases the ground in the same proportion: 6 AWG.

Result: two 4 AWG hots, a neutral of the same size and a 6 AWG ground, all copper, on a 60 A breaker.

Questions

What size wire for a 50 amp sub panel?

8 AWG copper or 6 AWG aluminum, on a 50 A breaker, up to 75 ft one way. At 100 ft the chart moves to 6 AWG copper to keep the drop within 3 % at a full 50 A on 240 V.

What size wire for a 60 amp sub panel?

6 AWG copper or 4 AWG aluminum, on a 60 A breaker, up to 100 ft one way. At 150 ft the chart moves to 4 AWG copper to keep the drop within 3 % at a full 60 A on 240 V.

What size wire for a 100 amp sub panel?

3 AWG copper or 1 AWG aluminum, on a 100 A breaker, up to 100 ft one way. At 150 ft the chart moves to 2 AWG copper to keep the drop within 3 % at a full 100 A on 240 V.

Why do other sites say 6 AWG for a 50 amp sub panel?

They size NM-B cable. NM-B (Romex) cable is held to the 60 °C column by NEC 334.80, where 8 AWG copper is only 40 A. On NM-B a 50 A feeder needs 6 AWG copper and a 60 A feeder 4 AWG. The 8 AWG answer is for THHN or XHHW-2 conductors in conduit on 75 °C terminals, where 8 AWG copper is 50 A.

Why do some charts show a smaller wire for 100 amps?

They use NEC 310.12, which allows smaller conductors for a dwelling's service and for the one feeder that carries the dwelling's whole load. A feeder to a subpanel that carries only part of the load is sized from Table 310.16, as on this page.

Does a sub panel need 3 wires or 4?

Four: two hots, a neutral and an equipment ground. The neutral and the ground are kept on separate bars in the subpanel, and the neutral is bonded to ground only at the service (NEC 250.24(B)). A subpanel in a detached building also gets its own grounding electrode (NEC 250.32).

Is the distance one way or the total wire length?

One way: from the main panel to the subpanel. The voltage drop formula already counts the current going out and coming back.

How are these sizes worked out?

First the smallest conductor whose ampacity in the 75 °C column of NEC Table 310.16 covers the feeder breaker. Then the size goes up until the voltage drop at the feeder's full rating is 3 % or less on 240 V, using copper or aluminum resistance at operating temperature (K = 12.96 Ω·cmil/ft for copper, 21.3 for aluminum). These are the formulas of the wire size calculator on this site and of the Electrician Calc app.
