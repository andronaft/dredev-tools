24 × 30 concrete slab

Cubic yards, 40, 60 and 80 lb bags and rebar for a 24 by 30 foot slab, at 4, 5, 6 and 8 inches thick. Change any number in the calculator.

Quick answer: a 24 × 30 ft slab is 720 sq ft. At 4 in thick it takes 8.89 cubic yards of concrete, 9.78 with 10 % extra: order 10 yards of ready-mix or 440 bags of 80 lb. At 6 in thick it takes 14.67 yards with the extra.

Worked example: a 24 × 30 ft slab, 4 in thick

  1. Area: 24 × 30 = 720 sq ft. Perimeter for the forms: 2 × (24 + 30) = 108 ft.
  2. Volume: 720 sq ft × 4 in ÷ 12 = 240.0 cu ft, ÷ 27 = 8.89 cu yd.
  3. With 10 % extra for uneven ground and spillage: 9.78 cu yd. Ready-mix is ordered by the quarter yard: 10 cu yd.
  4. In bags: 264.0 cu ft ÷ 0.60 = 440 bags of 80 lb, or ÷ 0.45 = 587 bags of 60 lb. Over a cubic yard, so ready-mix is usually less work than 440 bags.
  5. Rebar at 16 in on centre: 19 bars of 29.5 ft and 24 bars of 23.5 ft, cut from 70 sticks of 20 ft #4, 799 lb of steel.

Result: order 10 cu yd of ready-mix or 440 bags of 80 lb, and 70 sticks of #4 rebar.

Questions

How many yards of concrete do I need for a 24x30 slab?

8.89 cubic yards at 4 in thick, 11.11 at 5 in and 13.33 at 6 in, before waste. With 10 % extra, order 10 yards for a 4 in slab and 14.75 for a 6 in slab.

How many bags of concrete for a 24x30 slab?

At 4 in thick with 10 % extra: 440 bags of 80 lb, 587 bags of 60 lb or 880 bags of 40 lb. Over a cubic yard, so ready-mix is usually less work than 440 bags.

How much rebar do I need for a 24x30 slab?

A #4 grid at 16 in on centre takes 70 sticks of 20 ft (1,196 ft of bar, 799 lb). At 12 in on centre it is 92 sticks, at 24 in 48. Use the spacing on your plans.

How thick should a 24x30 slab be?

That depends on what it carries and on the ground under it, so take it from your plans, your engineer or the local building department. The table shows the concrete for 4, 5, 6 and 8 in so you can price each.

How much extra concrete should I order?

Estimating guides commonly add 5–10 % for spillage and an uneven subgrade, more for complicated forms or pumping. Running short in the middle of a pour costs far more than a little left over.
