What is particular about R-410A

A blend that is printed with one column

R-410A is a blend: EPA gives its GWP as calculated for the blend (US EPA, Technology Transitions GWP Reference Table (page updated 2 April 2026)). Arkema's chart still prints a single pressure column for it, so there is no bubble or dew column to choose from. The calculator shows one saturation temperature and uses it for both superheat and subcooling.

Much higher pressure than R-22

At 40 °F saturated, R-410A reads 118.8 psig where R-22 reads 68.6 psig; as 105 °F liquid it is 341.9 psig against 210.8 psig. One chart cannot stand in for the other: a 119 psig suction reading looked up on the R-22 chart comes out as 68.8 °F instead of 40.1 °F.

R-454B and R-32 are for new equipment

Arkema lists R-454B and R-32 as replacements for R-410A in new equipment (Arkema, 2025 Forane® Technical Digest). Both are A2L refrigerants, and Chemours states that A2L refrigerants are for new equipment only, not for retrofits (Chemours, Opteon™ XL41 (R-454B) Pressure-Temperature Guide for A/C), so an R-410A unit is not topped off with either. Their charts sit close to this one and are still different: at 119 psig the dew point of R-454B is 45.5 °F and R-32 is at 39.1 °F, against 40.1 °F here. See R-454B vs R-410A and R-32 vs R-410A.

Class, oil and GWP

R-410A is in safety class A1 (lower toxicity, no flame propagation) (ASHRAE Standard 34 class as listed by US EPA SNAP, Substitutes in Residential and Light Commercial Air Conditioning and Heat Pumps (page updated 19 March 2026)). Arkema names POE as its lubricant (Arkema, 2025 Forane® Technical Digest). Its 100-year GWP is 2,088 per US EPA.

R-410A worked examples and pressures

The readings below are a comfort-cooling example. They show the arithmetic; they are not charging targets.

Worked example: R-410A superheat

  1. Suction (low-side) gauge pressure: 119 psig.
  2. Saturation temperature at 119 psig in the chart: 40.1 °F.
  3. Suction line temperature near the service valve: 52 °F.
  4. Superheat = 52 − 40.1 = 11.9 °F.

Result: 11.9 °F of superheat.

Worked example: R-410A subcooling

  1. Liquid (high-side) gauge pressure: 366 psig.
  2. Saturation temperature at 366 psig in the chart: 109.9 °F.
  3. Liquid line temperature near the service valve: 100 °F.
  4. Subcooling = 109.9 − 100 = 9.9 °F.

Result: 9.9 °F of subcooling.

What should R-410A superheat and subcooling be?

There is no single correct number for a refrigerant. The target belongs to the equipment: a fixed-orifice (piston) system is charged to a target superheat from the manufacturer's charging table (target superheat calculator), and a TXV or EEV system to the target subcooling on the unit's data plate. Always use the manufacturer's numbers for the unit in front of you.

Questions

Does R-410A need the bubble or the dew column?

Neither. The source chart prints one pressure column for R-410A, and this calculator uses that one saturation temperature for superheat and for subcooling.

Can I read R-410A pressures on an R-22 chart?

No. R-410A is at 118.8 psig at 40 °F and R-22 at 68.6 psig. A 119 psig reading on the R-22 chart would show 68.8 °F instead of 40.1 °F.

Can I top off an R-410A system with R-454B or R-32?

No. Arkema lists both as R-410A replacements for new equipment, and Chemours states that A2L refrigerants are for new equipment only, not for retrofits.

Where does the data come from?

Arkema Forane® Pressure Temperature Chart (PPC/SDC 05-2025). Between the printed 5 °F points the calculator interpolates along the vapor-pressure curve.
