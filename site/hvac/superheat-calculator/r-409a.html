R-409A Superheat & Subcooling Calculator

Enter gauge pressure and line temperature for R-409A. Saturation comes from the published R-409A PT chart, with the dew point for superheat and the bubble point for subcooling.

Quick answer: with R-409A, superheat = suction line temperature − dew point at the suction pressure, and subcooling = bubble point at the liquid pressure − liquid line temperature. Example: 18 psig on the suction side is 20.0 °F saturated vapor, so a 32 °F suction line has 12.0 °F of superheat.

R-409A worked examples and saturation table

The readings below are a medium-temperature refrigeration example. They show the arithmetic; they are not charging targets.

Worked example: R-409A superheat

  1. Suction (low-side) gauge pressure: 18 psig.
  2. Saturation temperature at 18 psig in the vapor (dew) column: 20.0 °F.
  3. Suction line temperature near the service valve: 32 °F.
  4. Superheat = 32 − 20.0 = 12.0 °F.

Result: 12.0 °F of superheat.

Worked example: R-409A subcooling

  1. Liquid (high-side) gauge pressure: 159 psig.
  2. Saturation temperature at 159 psig in the liquid (bubble) column: 105.0 °F.
  3. Liquid line temperature near the service valve: 95 °F.
  4. Subcooling = 105.0 − 95 = 10.0 °F.

Result: 10.0 °F of subcooling.

What should R-409A superheat and subcooling be?

There is no single correct number for a refrigerant. The target belongs to the equipment: a fixed-orifice (piston) system is charged to a target superheat from the manufacturer's charging table (target superheat calculator), and a TXV or EEV system to the target subcooling on the unit's data plate. Always use the manufacturer's numbers for the unit in front of you.

Questions

How do I calculate superheat for R-409A?

Convert the suction pressure to the dew point (vapor column) with the R-409A PT chart and subtract it from the suction line temperature. Example: 18 psig is 20.0 °F, and a 32 °F line gives 12.0 °F of superheat.

How do I calculate subcooling for R-409A?

Convert the liquid pressure to the bubble point (liquid column) and subtract the liquid line temperature. Example: 159 psig is 105.0 °F, and a 95 °F liquid line gives 10.0 °F of subcooling.

What is the saturation temperature of R-409A at 18 psig?

20.0 °F dew point (vapor) and 5.3 °F bubble point (liquid).

Do I use the bubble or the dew column for R-409A?

Dew (vapor) for superheat, bubble (liquid) for subcooling. R-409A is a blend with glide: 14.4 °F at a 40 °F dew point and 12.4 °F at a 105 °F bubble point. Using the wrong column shifts the result by that much.

What superheat or subcooling should a R-409A system have?

It depends on the equipment, not on the refrigerant alone. Use the target superheat from the manufacturer's charging table for a fixed-orifice system, or the target subcooling on the data plate for a TXV or EEV system.

Where does the data come from?

Arkema Forane® Pressure-Temperature Wall Chart, Americas (2019). Between the printed 5 °F points the calculator interpolates along the vapor-pressure curve.
