Candle wax calculator

How much wax and fragrance oil one jar takes, and the totals for a batch, from the weight of water the jar holds to the fill line.

Quick answer: weigh the water your jar holds to the fill line and multiply by 0.86. A jar that holds 8 oz of water takes 6.88 oz of wax and fragrance: at 8 % that is 6.37 oz of wax and 0.51 oz of oil. Ten pounds of wax fills about 25 of those jars.

Worked example: twelve candles in jars that hold 8 oz of water

  1. Fill per candle: 8 oz of water × 0.86 = 6.88 oz of wax and fragrance.
  2. At 8 % of the wax: wax = 6.88 ÷ 1.08 = 6.37 oz, fragrance = 0.51 oz.
  3. Twelve candles with 5 % extra for the pot and spills: 80.3 oz of wax (5.02 lb) and 6.42 oz of fragrance.

Result: melt about 5.0 lb of wax and weigh 6.4 oz of fragrance oil.

Questions

How much wax do I need for an 8 oz jar?

If the jar holds 8 oz of water to your fill line, it takes 6.88 oz of wax and fragrance together. With an 8 % fragrance load that is 6.37 oz of wax and 0.51 oz of oil.

Why multiply by 0.86?

Wax is lighter than water: common candle waxes weigh 82–95 % as much as the same volume of water, and 0.86 is the usual starting value. Weigh a finished test candle to find the real factor for your wax.

How do I measure what my jar holds?

Put the empty jar on a scale, zero it and fill with water to the line where the wax will stop. The reading is the number to enter. Without a scale: 1 US fluid ounce of water weighs about 1.04 oz (29.6 g).

How many candles does 10 lb of wax make?

For jars that hold 8 oz of water at an 8 % load, each candle takes 6.37 oz of wax, so 10 lb (160 oz) fills about 25 candles before spills.

Is an 8 oz jar 8 oz of wax?

No. Jars are named by the volume they hold, not by the weight of wax. An 8 fl oz jar filled to the brim holds about 8.3 oz of water by weight, and you normally stop below the brim, so measure to your own fill line.
