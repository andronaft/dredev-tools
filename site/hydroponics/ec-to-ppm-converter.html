EC to ppm converter

Convert EC to ppm on the 500, 640 or 700 scale, or a ppm reading back to EC, and check it against a crop's published range.

Quick answer: ppm = EC (mS/cm) × 500, 640 or 700, depending on your meter's scale. 1.0 mS/cm is 500, 640 or 700 ppm; 1.8 mS/cm is 900, 1,152 or 1,260 ppm. EC is the real measurement, so compare readings in EC.

Which scale is my meter? Reading in 1,413 µS/cm calibration solution

ScaleFactorThe meter shows
500 scale× 0.50706.5 ppm
640 scale× 0.64904.3 ppm

Dip the meter in standard 1.413 mS/cm calibration solution: the ppm it shows tells you its factor.

Worked example: a reservoir at 1.8 mS/cm

  1. 1.8 mS/cm is 1,800 µS/cm.
  2. 500 scale: 1,800 × 0.5 = 900 ppm. 700 scale: 1,800 × 0.7 = 1,260 ppm.
  3. Going back: a feed chart that asks for 840 ppm on the 700 scale means 840 ÷ 700 = 1.2 mS/cm, which a 500-scale meter shows as 600 ppm.

Result: the same water reads 900 ppm on one meter and 1,260 ppm on another. Compare in EC.

Questions

How do I convert EC to ppm?

Multiply the EC in mS/cm by your meter's scale: × 500, × 640 or × 700. To go back, divide the ppm by the same number. 1.4 mS/cm is 700 ppm on the 500 scale and 980 ppm on the 700 scale.

Is my meter on the 500 or the 700 scale?

Check the manual, or dip the meter in 1.413 mS/cm calibration solution: about 706.5 ppm means the 500 scale, about 989.1 ppm the 700 scale. Hanna's guide gives 0.5 for meters calibrated with sodium chloride and 0.7 for the 442 type solutions that behave like fertilizer.

Why do two meters show different ppm in the same water?

Both measure the same conductivity and then multiply by a different factor. Nothing is wrong with either meter: they use different scales. That is why nutrient charts are easier to follow in EC.

Is ppm the real amount of nutrients in the water?

No. A TDS meter does not weigh anything: it measures how well the water conducts electricity and converts that to an estimate. Different salts conduct differently, so treat ppm as a convenient label for EC.

What EC should my nutrient solution be?

It depends on the crop: see the EC and pH chart by crop with the ranges published by Oklahoma State University Extension.
