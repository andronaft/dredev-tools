12 AWG voltage drop per foot

How many volts 12 AWG copper loses per foot at 5 to 20 A, how far you can run it, and the same figure for 14 to 2 AWG.

Quick answer: 12 AWG copper drops about 3.97 mV per amp per foot of one-way length (single phase or DC). At 20 A that is 7.94 V per 100 ft, so a 120 V circuit reaches 3 % at about 45 ft.

Checked 12 November 2026 by Andrii Zhukovskyi. Computed with K = 12.96 Ω·cmil/ft for copper: resistivity 0.017241 Ω·mm²/m at 20 °C plus 25 % for operating temperature, the resistance the voltage drop calculator on this site uses.

Worked example: 15 A on 12 AWG copper, 120 V, 80 ft

  1. Drop per amp per foot of one-way length: 3.971 mV.
  2. 3.971 mV × 15 A × 80 ft = 4.76 V.
  3. As a share of 120 V: 3.97 %.

Result: 4.76 V, over the 3 % recommendation.

Questions

What is the voltage drop of 12 AWG wire per foot?

About 3.97 millivolts per amp for each foot of one-way length on a single-phase or DC circuit (copper, both conductors counted). At 20 A that is 0.0794 V per foot, or 7.94 V per 100 ft.

How far can I run 12 AWG on a 20 amp circuit?

About 45 ft one way at 120 V and 91 ft at 240 V before the drop passes 3 % at a full 20 A. At 15 A the 120 V distance is about 60 ft.

What is the voltage drop on 100 ft of 12 AWG at 20 amps?

7.94 V: 6.6 % at 120 V or 3.3 % at 240 V.

Is 3 % a code requirement?

For most circuits it is a recommendation in the NEC's informational notes (3 % on a branch circuit, 5 % for feeder and branch together), not a rule. Some equipment and some local codes do require it.

How is this calculated?

VD = 2 × K × I × L ÷ CM, with 6,530 circular mils for 12 AWG (NEC Chapter 9, Table 8) and K = 12.96 Ω·cmil/ft for copper: resistivity 0.017241 Ω·mm²/m at 20 °C plus 25 % for operating temperature, the resistance the voltage drop calculator on this site uses. That is 2 × 12.964 ÷ 6,530 = 3.971 mV per amp per foot. Handbooks often round K to 12.9, which gives 3.951 mV, about 0.5 % less.
