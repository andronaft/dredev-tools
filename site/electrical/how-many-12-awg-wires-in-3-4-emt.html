How many 12 AWG wires in ¾ in. EMT?

The conduit fill count for 12 AWG and 10 AWG THHN in ½ and ¾ in. EMT, and the ampacity rule that usually stops you before the conduit is full.

Quick answer: 16 THHN conductors of 12 AWG fit in ¾ in. EMT at the 40 % fill limit, and 9 fit in ½ in. EMT. For 10 AWG THHN it is 10 and 5 (NEC Chapter 9, Tables 1, 4 and 5, 2023 edition).

Guide: how to calculate conduit fill for mixed wire sizes

That is fill only. With more than three current-carrying conductors in the conduit, NEC 310.15(C)(1) lowers the ampacity of each one: 12 AWG THHN stays good for 20 A circuits up to 9 current-carrying conductors, and from 10 up the circuits need 10 AWG.

Conduit fill: how many fit

THHN or THWN-2, all one size, 40 % fill for more than two conductors (NEC Chapter 9, Table 1). Grounds count toward fill.

¾ in. EMT has 0.533 in² inside (Table 4); 40 % of it is 0.2132 in². One 12 AWG THHN is 0.0133 in² (Table 5), and 0.2132 ÷ 0.0133 = 16.03, so 16 fit.

Ampacity: how many may carry current

Why other sites give a smaller number: 16 is how many wires physically fit. Once more than three of them carry current, NEC 310.15(C)(1) reduces the ampacity of every one, and from 10 current-carrying conductors up 12 AWG THHN no longer carries 20 A. That is why 9 is often given as the practical limit for 20 A circuits.

THHN is a 90 °C conductor, so the adjustment starts from the 90 °C column of Table 310.16 (30 A for 12 AWG, 40 A for 10 AWG) and the result may not exceed the 75 °C termination value (25 A and 35 A). 30 °C ambient; a hot attic or roof lowers these further. Breakers on 12 and 10 AWG copper stay at 20 and 30 A (240.4(D)).

Worked example: Five 20 A, 120 V circuits in one conduit

  1. Each two-wire circuit has a hot and a neutral, and both carry current: five circuits are 10 current-carrying conductors. The ground is not counted for ampacity.
  2. Table 310.15(C)(1), 10–20 conductors: multiply the ampacity by 0.50. 12 AWG THHN starts at 30 A in the 90 °C column, so it carries 15 A. Not enough for a 20 A circuit: the circuits need 10 AWG.
  3. Fill of ten 10 AWG and one 12 AWG ground: 10 × 0.0211 + 0.0133 = 0.2243 in². In ¾ in. EMT (0.533 in²) that is 42.1 %, over the 40 % limit; in 1 in. EMT (0.864 in²) it is 26.0 %.
  4. Four circuits stay at eight current-carrying conductors, factor 0.70: 12 AWG still carries 21 A, and eight 12 AWG with a 12 AWG ground fill ¾ in. EMT to 22.5 %.

Result: four 20 A circuits fit ¾ in. EMT on 12 AWG. A fifth circuit means 10 AWG and 1 in. EMT.

Questions

How many 12 AWG wires can I put in 3/4 EMT?

16 THHN or THWN-2 conductors by conduit fill. If they are 20 A circuits, no more than 9 of them may be current-carrying before the wire has to go up to 10 AWG.

How many 12 AWG wires fit in 1/2 EMT?

9 THHN conductors by fill. All 9 can carry current on 20 A circuits: with 7–9 current-carrying conductors 12 AWG THHN still carries 21 A.

How many 10 AWG wires fit in 3/4 EMT?

10 THHN conductors by fill, and 5 in ½ in. EMT. For 30 A circuits the ampacity limit comes first: with more than six current-carrying conductors a full 30 A load needs 8 AWG.

How many 20 amp circuits can I run in 3/4 conduit?

Four two-wire 120 V circuits on 12 AWG THHN: eight current-carrying conductors and a ground. A fifth circuit takes the count to ten, which needs 10 AWG and, with it, a larger conduit.

Do neutrals and grounds count?

For fill, every conductor counts, grounds included. For the ampacity adjustment, count the hots and the neutral of each two-wire circuit. A neutral that carries only the unbalanced current of a multiwire circuit is not counted (NEC 310.15(E)), and grounds are never counted (310.15(F)).

Where do these numbers come from?

NFPA 70, National Electrical Code, 2023 edition: Chapter 9, Tables 1, 4 and 5 for fill, Table 310.16 and Table 310.15(C)(1) for ampacity and its adjustment, 240.4(D) for the breaker limits on small conductors. They are the tables and formulas of the Electrician Calc app.
